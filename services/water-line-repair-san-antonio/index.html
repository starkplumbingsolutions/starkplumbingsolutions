Signs of a Damaged Water Supply Line

Watch for water pooling or soggy soil in the yard, a meter that keeps moving when every faucet is off, a sudden drop in pressure at every tap, a wet spot on the driveway or near the foundation, a water bill that climbs without any change in use, and cloudy or brown water after a leak. One sign can be a minor issue. Two or more together usually mean an underground leak that is getting worse.

Who Is Responsible: Public Mains vs. Your Service Line

San Antonio Water System (SAWS) maintains public water mains and the meter. From the meter to your home, the service line is usually the property owner's responsibility. Check your SAWS account and the meter location before you assume who pays. For public main or meter emergencies, call SAWS at 210-704-7297, available 24 hours a day. SAWS also runs the Plumbers to People program, which helps qualifying low-income residential customers with emergency fresh water leak repairs. Ask SAWS directly about eligibility.

Leak Detection and Pipe Diagnosis

Finding the leak is most of the job. We start with a meter test: shut off every fixture and any inside water use, then watch the meter's low-flow indicator. A moving indicator confirms a leak on the supply side. From there we pinpoint the line with pressure testing and listening equipment, and mark the route before digging so nothing else is damaged.

Water Line Replacement Methods

Open-trench repair is often the most direct and cost-effective option when the line is shallow and accessible. Trenchless methods such as pipe bursting or pipe lining can reduce yard disruption on longer runs or where driveways, landscaping or slabs are involved. Common service line materials include copper, PEX and PVC. We recommend the method that fits the break, the soil and your priorities, and we explain the tradeoffs in writing.

Water Line Repair vs. Replacement

A single clean break on a young line is usually a repair. Replacement makes sense when the line has repeated breaks, is galvanized steel, shows root intrusion or heavy corrosion, or sits in soil that keeps shifting. Replacing the run once often costs less than paying for repeat emergency calls.

New Water Service Line Installation

We also install new service lines for new construction, relocations and remodels. The line is sized for your fixtures and pressure, and the connection to the meter and main is coordinated with the utility's requirements.

Don't dig or DIY a water line. Digging near a live supply line can cause flooding, damage gas or electrical utilities, and violate permit rules. Cutting or tapping a pressurized line without the right fittings can burst it. Let a licensed plumber locate and repair the line.

Seasonal Issues in San Antonio

  • Summer: drought and heat shrink clay soil, and cracks can open on supply lines. Watch for sudden pressure drops.
  • Spring: heavy rain saturates soil and exposes weak joints and shallow lines.
  • Winter: hard freezes are rare here but can split exposed or shallow lines. Wrap exposed runs and know where your shutoff is.
  • Year-round: tree roots reach water lines in search of moisture. Watch for soft, wet spots near large trees.

Before the Plumber Arrives: Checklist

Having these ready shortens the visit and helps us quote accurately.

  • Locate your water meter and main shutoff valve
  • Photograph the meter and read it with all water off
  • Save the last 12 months of water bills
  • Note where you see wet spots and when they started
  • Mark sprinkler lines and known utility lines in the yard
  • Keep your SAWS account number handy

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is water pooling in my yard?

Pooling often means a supply line leak under the lawn, but sprinkler overspray, a clogged drain or a drainage problem can look similar. A meter check and leak detection tell the difference.

Who repairs an underground water service line?

A licensed plumber repairs the private service line from the meter to the house. SAWS repairs public mains and the meter. If the leak is on the public side, call SAWS at 210-704-7297.

Can a water line be replaced without disturbing the entire yard?

Often yes. Trenchless methods or a targeted trench can limit disruption. We discuss the options and expected restoration before work begins.

Does insurance cover underground water line repair?

It depends on your policy and the cause. Many standard policies exclude underground pipe failure unless extra coverage was added. Check with your insurer and keep records of the leak and the repair.
