Signs of a Damaged Water Supply Line

Watch for water pooling or soggy soil in the yard, a meter that keeps moving when every faucet is off, a sudden drop in pressure at every tap, a wet spot on the driveway or near the foundation, a water bill that climbs without any change in use, and cloudy or brown water after a leak. One sign can be a minor issue. Two or more together usually mean an underground leak that is getting worse.

♨️

Who Is Responsible: Public Mains vs. Your Service Line

San Antonio Water System (SAWS) maintains public water mains and the meter. From the meter to your home, the service line is usually the property owner's responsibility. Check your SAWS account and the meter location before you assume who pays. For public main or meter emergencies, call SAWS at 210-704-7297, available 24 hours a day. SAWS also runs the Plumbers to People program, which helps qualifying low-income residential customers with emergency fresh water leak repairs. Ask SAWS directly about eligibility.

💧

Leak Detection and Pipe Diagnosis

Finding the leak is most of the job. We start with a meter test: shut off every fixture and any inside water use, then watch the meter's low-flow indicator. A moving indicator confirms a leak on the supply side. From there we pinpoint the line with pressure testing and listening equipment, and mark the route before digging so nothing else is damaged.

🔥

Water Line Replacement Methods

Open-trench repair is often the most direct and cost-effective option when the line is shallow and accessible. Trenchless methods such as pipe bursting or pipe lining can reduce yard disruption on longer runs or where driveways, landscaping or slabs are involved. Common service line materials include copper, PEX and PVC. We recommend the method that fits the break, the soil and your priorities, and we explain the tradeoffs in writing.

🧪

Water Line Repair vs. Replacement

A single clean break on a young line is usually a repair. Replacement makes sense when the line has repeated breaks, is galvanized steel, shows root intrusion or heavy corrosion, or sits in soil that keeps shifting. Replacing the run once often costs less than paying for repeat emergency calls.

⚖️

New Water Service Line Installation

We also install new service lines for new construction, relocations and remodels. The line is sized for your fixtures and pressure, and the connection to the meter and main is coordinated with the utility's requirements.

🛠️

How to Do a Meter Leak Test Before You Call

You can gather useful evidence in ten minutes. Turn off every faucet and appliance that uses water, including the ice maker and irrigation, then open the meter box and look at the low-flow indicator, the small triangle or dial that spins with even tiny flow. If it moves, water is flowing somewhere. Close your main shutoff valve at the house and look again. If the indicator stops, the leak is inside the house or past the valve. If it keeps moving, the leak is between the meter and the shutoff, which means the service line. Write down the time and reading, and send us a photo.

Do not open the utility's valve or pry at the meter. Use only your own shutoff valve. The meter belongs to the utility.

📋

SAWS Leak Letters and High Water Bills

A common worry on neighborhood groups is a letter from SAWS saying there may be a leak while the homeowner cannot find one. Start with the usual hidden culprits: a running toilet, a leaking outdoor faucet or hose bib, an irrigation zone stuck on, and a leaking water softener or water heater drain line. If those check out and the meter still moves with everything off, the leak is likely underground. Our leak detection pinpoints it so you can repair the right spot and ask SAWS about any billing adjustment policy. Call SAWS at 210-704-7297 for questions about your account and public-side issues.

🧰

If the Problem Began After Meter Work

Several residents report a leak that appeared right after the utility replaced a meter. If that happens, photograph the area before anyone touches it, note the date and work order number, and keep a copy of your bills. Report the issue to SAWS in writing and ask who is responsible for the connection. We can inspect the fitting and give you a written description of what we find. That documentation helps whichever party ends up responsible, and it keeps a dispute from turning into a guess.

💡

Service Line Materials: Copper, PEX, PVC and Galvanized

Older homes may have galvanized steel, which corrodes from the inside and restricts flow. Copper is durable but can suffer pinholes in aggressive water. PEX is flexible and resists scale and freezing, and it can be pulled through a trenchless path. Rigid plastic is common on some service runs. The right material depends on local code, soil, run length, depth and whether the line is under a slab, a driveway or a yard. We explain the choice in writing so you know what is going into the ground.

🏠

Trench or Trenchless: What It Means for Your Yard

Open trench repair exposes the whole line and lets us inspect fittings and bedding. It also means digging, so we protect landscaping where possible, restore backfill and flag what restoration you will want to handle. Trenchless options, such as pipe bursting or lining, use small access pits instead of a continuous trench. They help when the path crosses a driveway, a patio, a mature tree or a slab. Not every line qualifies, and costs differ, so we compare both approaches for your property in writing.

🔧

Water Pressure, Water Hammer and Line Damage

High supply pressure and sudden valve closures can stress a service line and the fittings on it. Banging pipes, a failing pressure regulator and sudden pressure swings can all shorten line life. Replacing a bad regulator is cheaper than replacing a line, so we check pressure during any water line visit. See our Water Pressure Repair and Regulator Replacement pages for related help.

🔍

What Changes the Price of a Water Line Repair

Depth, length, soil, access, material, surface restoration and whether a permit or utility locate is required all affect cost. A short exposed repair near the meter is a different job from replacing a line under a driveway. Request a written estimate that separates leak location, excavation, materials, restoration and any permit costs. If two quotes differ widely, compare what each includes, because the cheaper one may leave out restoration or fittings.

♨️

Protecting Your Water Line for the Long Term

You cannot make a buried line last forever, but you can improve the odds. Keep a regulator in good working order so pressure stays in a safe range. Avoid planting large trees near the line. Mark the shutoff valve and test it once a year so it does not seize. Keep irrigation lines and sprinkler heads in good repair, since leaks near the line can wash away soil support. Read your meter monthly. A simple habit of writing down the reading and comparing it to your bill catches leaks early.

💧

Roots, Soil Movement and San Antonio Clay

San Antonio soils swell when wet and shrink in dry spells, and that movement stresses buried pipes and the fittings on them. Long dry periods can open cracks in clay and drop foundation support, and heavy rain after a drought can shift the ground again. Tree roots seek water and can wrap around or push against lines. When a line fails near a mature tree or near a foundation crack, we look for these causes so the repair does not fail again in the same place. We discuss sleeving, rerouting or replacing a section when the pattern suggests the soil is part of the problem.

🔥

The First Fifteen Minutes of a Broken Water Line

Stay calm and move in order. First, find your main shutoff at the house and turn it off. If water keeps flowing from the yard, the break is before the shutoff, so look for the meter valve, but do not operate the utility's valve yourself. Second, turn off the water heater so the tank does not run dry, and shut off electricity to any area with standing water if it is safe to do so. Third, take photos and video for your records. Fourth, call us for a private line break, or SAWS at 210-704-7297 for a public main or meter emergency. Fifth, move valuables and keep people and pets away from flooded areas and electrical equipment. Do not dig or cut anything until the lines are located.

🧪

Water Line Repair for Landlords, Property Managers and Small Businesses

If you manage rentals or a small commercial property, a broken water line can mean a tenant without water and a bill that climbs by the hour. Call us with the property address, the meter location and any prior repair history. We confirm the leak, give a written estimate for approval, and send a report with photos and findings that you can use for your records, your insurer or your owner. We also coordinate access with tenants so repairs move quickly and without surprises.

⚖️

Keep Records of Every Water Line Repair

Save the invoice, photos, permit record and warranty information. Note the date, location and material used. These records help if the line fails again, if you sell the home, or if an insurer or the utility asks what happened. We include a written summary with every repair so your file is complete.

Before the Plumber Arrives

Having these ready shortens the visit and helps us quote accurately.

  • Locate your water meter and main shutoff valve
  • Photograph the meter and read it with all water off
  • Save the last 12 months of water bills
  • Note where you see wet spots and when they started
  • Mark sprinkler lines and known utility lines in the yard
  • Keep your SAWS account number handy

Seasonal Issues in San Antonio

  • Summer: drought and heat shrink clay soil, and cracks can open on supply lines. Watch for sudden pressure drops.
  • Spring: heavy rain saturates soil and exposes weak joints and shallow lines.
  • Winter: hard freezes are rare here but can split exposed or shallow lines. Wrap exposed runs and know where your shutoff is.
  • Year-round: tree roots reach water lines in search of moisture. Watch for soft, wet spots near large trees.

Questions to Ask Any Plumber

Use these with every company you call, including us.

  • Will you confirm the leak location before digging?
  • Who handles utility locates and permits?
  • Is yard or driveway restoration included in the price?
  • What material and fittings will you use?
  • Do you offer a trenchless option for my property?
  • Will you document the repair for my records and insurer?

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Digging before the lines are located
  • Using unrated fittings from a hardware store on a pressurized line
  • Assuming the utility fixes every leak on your side of the meter
  • Ignoring a small wet spot that grows after rain
  • Throwing away bills that show the first sign of the leak
  • Hiring based on one price without comparing what is included

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is water pooling in my yard?

Pooling often means a supply line leak under the lawn, but sprinkler overspray, a clogged drain or a drainage problem can look similar. A meter check and leak detection tell the difference.

Who repairs an underground water service line?

A licensed plumber repairs the private service line from the meter to the house. SAWS repairs public mains and the meter. If the leak is on the public side, call SAWS at 210-704-7297.

Can a water line be replaced without disturbing the entire yard?

Often yes. Trenchless methods or a targeted trench can limit disruption. We discuss the options and expected restoration before work begins.

Does insurance cover underground water line repair?

It depends on your policy and the cause. Many standard policies exclude underground pipe failure unless extra coverage was added. Check with your insurer and keep records of the leak and the repair.

How much does it cost to repair a water line in San Antonio?

It depends on depth, length, access, material and restoration. A short exposed repair costs far less than replacing a long line under a driveway. We give a written estimate that itemizes the work.

What are the signs of a broken water main to my house?

Soggy ground, low pressure at every tap, a meter that runs with everything off, a sudden jump in your bill, or the sound of running water when nothing is on.

Is there help for low-income homeowners with a water leak?

SAWS offers the Plumbers to People program for qualifying residential customers. Contact SAWS to ask about eligibility and how to apply.

Can I stop the leak myself until you arrive?

Turn off the main shutoff at the house. If the leak is before the shutoff, leave the utility's meter valve alone and call us or SAWS.
