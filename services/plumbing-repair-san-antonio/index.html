Common Household Plumbing Problems

Most San Antonio plumbing calls come down to a short list of problems. Knowing which one you have helps you describe it and helps us quote it accurately.

  • Leaks at supply lines, shutoff valves, under sinks, or inside walls.
  • Running or weak-flush toilets caused by worn internal parts.
  • Dripping or loose faucets and showerheads.
  • Low or uneven water pressure from scale, a failing regulator or a hidden leak.
  • Slow drains and backups in one fixture or several.
  • Noisy pipes such as banging when a valve closes.

San Antonio water is very hard, and homeowners commonly report levels in the range of 15 to 22 grains per gallon. Scale builds up inside fixtures, valves and water heaters and is behind a lot of repeat repairs.

Pipe and Water Line Repairs

Pinhole leaks, corroded joints and cracked lines are common in older homes and in areas where shifting clay soil stresses buried pipe. A small leak that is ignored can soak framing, flooring and insulation long before it shows on the surface.

We find the exact failure point first. Where one section is bad and the rest of the pipe is sound, a spot repair is the honest answer. Where the pipe is failing along its length, we explain why and show you the options, including Water Line Repair & Replacement or Whole-House Repiping.

Not sure which of these matches your problem? Describe it and we will point you to the right fix.

Drain and Sewer Problems

One slow fixture is usually a local clog. Several fixtures slow or gurgling together, or sewage smells and backups, point to the main line. That difference decides the fix and the cost, so we sort it out before quoting.

Start with Drain Cleaning for local clogs, and Sewer Line Repair if the main line is damaged.

Plumbing Leak Diagnosis

A leak you can see is rarely the whole story. We trace the leak to its source so you are not paying to repair the wrong spot.

Quick meter check you can do now: turn off every faucet and appliance that uses water, then look at your water meter. If the leak indicator or dial is still moving, water is escaping somewhere. San Antonio Water System customers can also watch for a continuous-use alert from their ConnectH2O meter.

If the meter confirms a hidden leak, go to Water Leak Detection.

What Does Plumbing Repair Cost in San Antonio?

Plumbing repair prices vary a lot because the same symptom can have a ten-minute fix or a half-day fix. We do not guess a number over the phone for a problem we have not seen. You get the price in writing after we diagnose.

What moves the price

  • How hard the problem is to reach (open under-sink vs. inside a wall or under a slab).
  • Parts needed and whether your fixture is standard or specialty.
  • How long diagnosis takes, especially for hidden leaks.
  • Whether permits or specialist licensing are required.

How to avoid overpaying

  • Be wary of a number quoted without anyone seeing the problem.
  • Ask for the price in writing before work starts, with labor, parts and any trip or diagnostic fee listed separately.
  • Ask the plumber to explain the cause in plain words. If you get a vague answer, get a second opinion.
  • Compare what is included, not just the headline price. A cheap special that grows into a long add-on list is not cheap.
  • Never let scope change on the fly. If something new is found, work should stop until you approve it.
  • Ask what happens if the problem comes back, and make sure anything promised is on the estimate.

What to do right now if you have a plumbing problem

  1. Find your main water shutoff valve and make sure everyone in the house knows where it is.
  2. Shut off the local valve under the sink or behind the toilet if the leak is at a fixture.
  3. Move belongings away from wet areas and put a bucket or towels under active drips.
  4. Take a photo or short video of the problem, then call or text us.
  5. Do not pour chemical drain openers into a slow drain you plan to have a plumber look at.
Smell gas or see sewage flooding? Leave the area, avoid switches and flames, and call your gas utility or 911 first.

Plumbing Repair FAQs

What plumbing problems require professional repair?

Call a plumber for hidden or recurring leaks, low pressure throughout the house, sewage backups, no hot water, gas odors, anything involving the main water line, and any repair you cannot reach or shut off safely. Simple fixes like replacing a flapper can be DIY, but if the problem returns, it needs a diagnosis.

Why is my plumbing leaking?

Common causes are worn washers and seals, loose or corroded connections, high water pressure, hard-water scale, shifting soil stressing buried lines, and aging pipe. The visible drip is often downstream of the real cause, which is why a proper diagnosis matters.

When should plumbing be replaced?

Consider replacement when you have repeated pinhole leaks in different places, rust-colored water, steadily dropping pressure, or old galvanized or failing pipe. Replacing a single failed section is fine when the rest of the pipe is sound. We tell you which case you are in.

Will I know the price before work starts?

Yes. You get a written estimate listing labor, parts and any trip or diagnostic fee, and we wait for your approval. If we find something that changes the scope, we stop and talk to you first.

How do I check that a plumber is licensed in Texas?

Ask for the license number and look it up with the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners at tsbpe.texas.gov. Also ask for proof of liability insurance before any work begins.
