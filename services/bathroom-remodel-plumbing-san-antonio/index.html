Plumbing for Bathroom Renovations

A bathroom remodel is mostly a plumbing project that happens to have tile on it. The look is the vanity, tile and fixtures. What decides whether it lasts, and whether it stays on budget, is what is behind the walls and under the floor.

Homeowners who search for bathroom remodel plumbing in San Antonio are usually trying to answer one of four questions. Can I move this fixture? What will it cost? Who should I hire first? How do I avoid being burned by a bad contractor? Reading local community posts, the same frustrations repeat: fixtures loose or uneven after install, sinks that barely hold water, tub pans that were not tested, wet mortar beds sealed before they dried, and rough quotes that ballooned.

This page is about renovation projects, not single fixture repairs. If you only need a leaking faucet or running toilet, see our bathroom plumbing page or toilet repair service.

What bathroom remodel plumbing covers

  • Removing old fixtures and capping lines safely
  • Replacing worn supply lines, shutoffs and drain pipe
  • Moving or adding toilets, vanities, tubs, showers and floor drains
  • Setting new shower and tub valves at the right depth for your tile
  • Rerouting drains and vents to new layouts
  • Water and leak testing before walls and floors are closed
  • Final fixture setting and function testing
The most common regret: hiring the tile crew first and the plumber second. Your plumber should see the layout while walls are open.

Shower and Bathtub Installation

Showers and tubs are where remodel plumbing succeeds or fails, because failures stay hidden until water damage shows up.

Tub-to-shower conversions

Replacing a tub with a walk-in shower is one of the most popular remodels in San Antonio, especially for aging-in-place. It needs a new valve (often relocated higher), a shower drain set at the right slope, and a waterproof pan or curb. Because the drain location usually changes, the slab or subfloor often must be opened.

Valves and trim

Rough-in valves must match your trim kit brand and depth. We confirm model numbers before the wall is closed and set valve depth to your tile thickness. A mismatched valve means opening finished tile.

Pans, liners and the leak test

A shower pan is only as good as its test. A proper process is to flood-test the pan (fill it to the drain's flood level and hold for a set time) before tile goes down. One recurring horror story in local groups: a membrane applied over a wet mortar bed that never fully dried, trapping moisture and causing failures. Always ask who is responsible for the pan and how it is tested.

Don’t DIY this. Waterproofing, pan building and valve setting look easy on video, but failure shows up as stained ceilings below, mold or a rotted subfloor. Hire licensed pros and insist on a documented flood test.

Freestanding tubs and soaking tubs

Freestanding tubs need a floor-mounted or wall-mounted filler, a drain at the correct offset and often reinforced floor framing for weight. Plan these before framing is closed.

Toilet and Vanity Relocation

Can a toilet be moved? Yes. The constraints are slope, venting and structure.

A toilet needs a 3 or 4 inch drain with proper slope and a vent. On a slab foundation, moving it far means cutting the concrete, trenching, installing new pipe and patching. On a pier-and-beam home or a second floor, moving is easier because joists and crawlspace allow access, but joist cutting must be done carefully to protect structure.

Vanity and sink moves

Vanity moves are usually simpler than toilet moves. They involve supply stops, a 1.5 or 2 inch drain and a vent. Double-sink conversions add a second drain and often a wider vanity drain trap arrangement.

  • Keep wet walls shared when possible to cut cost
  • Choose the toilet before rough-in, since rough-in distance (10, 12 or 14 inches) matters
  • Consider ADA-height or comfort-height toilets and grab-bar blocking
  • Leave access to shutoffs, not tile over them

Water Supply and Drain Rerouting

Older San Antonio homes have surprises. Houses in central neighborhoods may have cast iron drains that are corroded, and galvanized supply lines that restrict flow. Mid-era homes may have polybutylene or early CPVC. Newer homes may have PEX.

A remodel is the cheapest time to fix them, because walls are already open. We inspect what we find and tell you what is worth replacing now.

What we check when walls are open

  • Condition and material of supply lines
  • Corrosion, cracks or scale in drain pipe
  • Existing venting and whether it meets current code
  • Hidden leaks, past repairs and old patches
  • Shutoff valves that no longer close
  • Water pressure at the fixture

Rerouting for new layouts

New layouts need new routes. We run supply in the most direct, protected path and drain pipe with correct slope. If we recommend replacing a section, we explain why, show you the pipe and price it separately so you can decide.

If old pipe is failing, it is far cheaper to replace it now than to demolish new tile later. See our whole-house repiping page for larger replacement projects.

Bathroom Plumbing Rough-Ins and Remodel Timeline

Here is the order work usually happens. Knowing it helps you schedule tile, glass and cabinetry without waiting on plumbing.

  1. Walkthrough and scope

    We look at the layout, discuss what you want to move or keep, and write up scope and price.

  2. Demolition and exposure

    Old fixtures and surfaces come out. We inspect pipes, venting and subfloor.

  3. Rough-in

    New drains, vents, supply lines and valves are installed to your layout.

  4. Testing

    We pressure-test supply, water-test drains and flood-test shower pans where applicable.

  5. Inspection

    If a permit applies to the work, the city inspects rough plumbing before walls close.

  6. Close-in and finishes

    Backer board, waterproofing, tile and cabinetry go in.

  7. Final fixture connections

    We set toilets, faucets, shower trim and drains, then test everything and show you the shutoffs.

Final fixture connections and testing

After tile, paint and cabinetry, we return to set fixtures. We check that toilets are firm and level, faucets run on correct hot and cold sides, drains clear quickly, no connection weeps under pressure, and shutoffs work. We leave you with a short note on what we installed and how to shut off water in an emergency.

Remodel Horror Stories and How to Avoid Them

Reddit and Facebook threads about San Antonio bathroom remodels read like a checklist of what not to accept. The complaints are consistent, and so are the solutions.

Common complaints

  • Fixtures installed loose or uneven. Ask for a final walk-through with each fixture tested.
  • Sinks that could barely hold water. Test every drain and stopper before sign-off.
  • Moisture trapped under waterproofing. Insist on dry substrates and documented flood tests.
  • Big upfront payments, then no-shows. Pay by milestone, and verify licenses.
  • Price jumps mid-job. Get an itemized scope and agree how unknowns will be priced.
  • Shoddy work found years later in the ceiling. Demand permits and photos of work before closing walls.

How we protect you

Written scope, itemized pricing, photos of rough-in before cover, documented tests and a clear final walkthrough. We also tell you when the right answer is a smaller scope.

San Antonio Seasons and Bathroom Remodels

Fall and winter are peak remodel seasons because families want finished bathrooms before holiday guests. Book plumbing early, since tile, glass and vanity lead times add weeks. Cold snaps also matter: exposed supply lines in exterior walls need insulation, which a remodel is a great time to add.

Spring brings rain and humidity. Open walls and unsealed pans need protection from moisture, and humidity affects drying time for mortar and waterproofing.

Summer heat dries concrete and mortar quickly but makes attic work harder. It is also when foundation movement can stress plumbing under slabs.

Hard water year-round. San Antonio water leaves mineral scale that clogs aerators, valves and shower heads. A remodel is the best time to install a softener connection. See water softener installation.

Materials, Code and What Gets Replaced

Supply pipe

Most remodels use PEX or copper for new supply runs. We tie into existing lines with transition fittings suited to the old material, so you do not mix incompatible metals in ways that corrode. Galvanized steel should be replaced where possible because it restricts flow and rusts internally.

Drain pipe

PVC or ABS is common for drains. Where old cast iron is failing, we transition to plastic with approved couplings. Floor drains for curbless showers need correct slope toward the drain, usually a quarter inch per foot, and proper subfloor framing.

Valves and shutoffs

Replace old angle stops and supply lines during a remodel. Aging stops fail when turned, and rubber supply lines burst. Quarter-turn valves and braided stainless lines are inexpensive insurance.

Venting

Every fixture needs proper venting. If a layout change moves the toilet, shower or vanity away from the existing vent, we add or reroute vents rather than relying on shortcuts that cause gurgling.

Water heater capacity

Rain showers, body sprays and soaking tubs can exceed what an old water heater supplies. We assess whether your heater and supply lines can handle the new demand. See water heater installation.

Accessibility and aging in place

Curbless showers, comfort-height toilets and grab-bar blocking are best planned before walls close. Ask us to note blocking locations so your carpenter can add them.

Why Bathroom Remodel Plumbing Should Not Be DIY

Replacing a faucet is DIY-friendly. Remodel plumbing is not.

Don’t DIY this. Relocating drains, setting shower valves, building pans and cutting into slabs are failure-prone jobs. A small error can leak for months inside a wall before you see a stain.
  • Hidden damage: Leaks behind tile can rot framing and grow mold.
  • Code and permits: Moved drains and vents require code-compliant sizing and slope.
  • Insurance: Unpermitted, unlicensed work can complicate claims.
  • Cost of redo: Re-tiling to fix a bad valve costs many times more than doing it right.

You can still save money by choosing finishes, handling demolition with your contractor and ordering fixtures on time. Let a licensed plumber handle the pipes.

Bathroom Remodel Plumbing FAQs: Straight Answers for San Antonio Homeowners

Can a toilet be moved during remodeling?

Yes. A toilet can usually be relocated if the new spot can receive a properly sloped and vented drain. On a slab foundation this often means cutting concrete, which adds cost. Raised foundations and second floors are usually easier.

Can a bathtub be replaced with a shower?

Yes. Tub-to-shower conversions need a new valve, shower drain and waterproof pan or curb. Because the drain location often changes, the floor may need to be opened.

When should a plumber be involved in a bathroom remodel?

Before demolition ends and before tile is ordered. Early input helps confirm what can be moved, which fixtures and valves to choose, and what the plumbing will cost.

How much does bathroom remodel plumbing cost in San Antonio?

It depends on how many fixtures move, whether the floor is a slab, and what condition the old pipes are in. We provide itemized written estimates after seeing the space or plans.

Do bathroom remodels require a plumbing permit?

Moving, adding or replacing drain, vent and supply piping generally requires a permit in San Antonio. Like-for-like fixture swaps may not. We confirm for your scope and handle permits for our work.

How long does bathroom remodel plumbing take?

Plumbing rough-in for a typical bathroom often takes a day or two, plus final fixture setting after finishes. Slab cuts, repiping or permit inspections can add time.

Will you work with my contractor?

Yes. We coordinate with general contractors and tile crews, and we also work directly with homeowners.

What is a shower pan flood test?

It is a test where the shower pan is filled with water to check for leaks before tile is installed. It is a key quality step that prevents hidden leaks.

Should I replace old pipes during a remodel?

If pipes are corroded, leaking or restricting flow, replacing them while walls are open is usually far cheaper than doing it later.
