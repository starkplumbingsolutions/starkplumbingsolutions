Outdoor Water Supply Lines

A backyard water line is not just pipe in a trench. It needs the right material (PEX, copper or PVC where code allows), correct burial depth, a shut-off you can reach, backflow protection where required and sensible routing away from trees, driveways and the foundation. Shifting clay soil in Bexar County flexes rigid lines over time, so fittings and depth matter.

What a proper install includes

  • Locating utilities before any digging (call 811 is required in Texas)
  • Tapping the supply in an accessible spot with a dedicated shut-off
  • Pressure-rated pipe, protected where it exits the ground or slab
  • A pressure test before we cover the trench
  • Backfill and surface restoration

Hose Bibs and Outdoor Faucets

Hose bibs fail in three ways: worn internals that drip, a cracked body from a freeze or an attached hose, and a failed supply pipe behind the wall. The third is the one that does real damage because water runs inside the wall cavity. See our dedicated outdoor faucet repair page for the full breakdown.

Garage and Outdoor Shower Plumbing

Garages and outdoor showers add fixtures far from your main plumbing. We run supply lines, add isolation valves so you can winterize the area alone and make sure waste goes where code allows. Outdoor showers in particular need a legal drain, not a pool of water against the slab.

  • Hot and cold supply with a mixing valve or shower valve rated for outdoor use
  • Isolation valve inside the house or garage for winter shut-down
  • Proper drain connection or approved dispersal, never against the foundation

Outdoor Plumbing Leaks

Exterior leaks hide. Watch for these signs:

  • Lush, greener or soggy grass in a strip or patch with no rain
  • Water meter spinning with every fixture off
  • Sudden jump in your SAWS bill
  • Reduced pressure at the hose bib or in the house
  • Damp or stained exterior wall, or warped trim below a spigot
  • The sound of running water when everything is off

Who do I call, SAWS or a plumber? Roughly: SAWS owns the line up to and including the meter. Everything from the meter to your house and fixtures is yours. If outdoor water keeps flowing after your house shut-off is closed, shut the meter valve if you can and call us; we will tell you quickly which side the leak is on.

Freeze Damage Prevention

San Antonio freezes are infrequent but severe because most homes are not built for them. Exposed hose bibs, backflow assemblers, irrigation lines and any pipe on an exterior wall are the first to split.

  • Disconnect hoses and cover bibs before the first freeze
  • Insulate exposed pipe, backflow assemblies and irrigation lines
  • Know your main shut-off before you need it
  • If a pipe splits, shut water off and call right away

See burst pipe repair for emergencies.

Don't DIY this

  • Dig near utilities without calling 811 first
  • Run a pipe in the yard and bury it without a pressure test
  • Splice rigid pipe back together over a leak that has not been located
  • Use thread tape or sealant as a permanent fix for a cracked body
  • Tie new outdoor fixtures into old galvanized lines without an assessment

FAQs

Can a plumber install a new backyard water line?

Yes. A licensed plumber can tap your supply, run pressure-rated pipe to the backyard, add a shut-off valve and pressure test the line. Utility locates are required before digging. Call (210) 898-9754 for a quote.

Why does an outdoor faucet leak behind the wall?

Usually a freeze crack in the bib body or the pipe just inside the wall, or a hose left attached that trapped water. The leak shows up when the faucet is on or after thaw. Stop using it and call a plumber; hidden wall water can damage drywall and insulation.

What outdoor pipes are vulnerable during a freeze?

Hose bibs, exposed backflow preventers, irrigation lines, pool and outdoor kitchen supply, and any pipe in an uninsulated garage, attic or exterior wall.
