Signs of a Slab Leak

  • Unexplained spike in the water bill, or a meter that moves when everything is off.
  • Warm or wet spots on concrete or finished floors.
  • Musty odors, mold or damp carpet near walls.
  • Low water pressure or the faint sound of running water when faucets are closed.
  • New cracks in walls or floors near the leak.

Confirm it with the meter test first. See Water Leak Detection.

Why Water Pipes Leak Beneath Concrete

Pipes under a slab are exposed to constant pressure, movement and chemistry.

  • Shifting clay soil flexes the slab and stresses pipe and joints.
  • Corrosion from aggressive water or aging copper.
  • Abrasion where pipe rubs against concrete or gravel.
  • Poor installation or fittings that fail with age.
  • High water pressure that accelerates wear.

Slab Leak Diagnosis

We confirm the leak with pressure and isolation tests, then locate it with electronic leak detection. The aim is to find the leak within a small area, so any repair is targeted.

Specialists in San Antonio use pressure testing, isolation and electronic tools, and so do we. We tell you whether the leak is on a pressurized water line or on a gravity sewer drain, since the repair options are different. For sewer drains, see Sewer Line Repair.

Direct Pipe Repair vs. Rerouting

There are three common ways to fix a slab leak, and each has trade-offs. We explain which one fits your home and why.

Repairing Hot and Cold Water Slab Leaks

Hot water lines tend to fail first because heat accelerates corrosion and expansion. A hot leak shows up as a warm spot and a high gas or electric bill as the heater runs constantly. Cold leaks are quieter and may show only as a high water bill and damp soil or flooring.

Where a line is old and has leaked more than once, repiping part or all of the system may cost less over time than repeated slab repairs. See Whole-House Repiping.

Protecting Flooring and Foundations

A slab leak left alone can soften soil under the foundation, damage flooring and feed mold. The sooner it is found, the smaller the repair. We protect finished floors and furniture during access, and explain restoration before we start.

If you see widening cracks, doors that stick or floors that slope, you may want a structural opinion as well as a plumber. We tell you what we see and where our work ends.

What Does Slab Leak Repair Cost in San Antonio?

Slab leak cost depends mostly on method and access. A reroute is often cheaper than tunneling or breaking concrete, and an under-slab sewer drain with tunneling is usually a bigger job than a water supply reroute. Homeowners in local forums report quotes ranging widely, with large bills where tunneling is involved. We give you the options and the price in writing.

What moves the price

  • Hot or cold line, supply or sewer.
  • Depth, location and how many leaks there are.
  • Method: reroute, tunnel or break the slab.
  • Flooring restoration, permits and access.
  • Whether a larger repipe makes more sense.

How to avoid overpaying

  • Ask for the leak location in writing and ask whether the quote covers floor or surface restoration.
  • Ask for the price in writing before work starts, with labor, parts and any trip or diagnostic fee listed separately.
  • Ask the plumber to explain the cause in plain words. If you get a vague answer, get a second opinion.
  • Compare what is included, not just the headline price. A cheap special that grows into a long add-on list is not cheap.
  • Never let scope change on the fly. If something new is found, work should stop until you approve it.
  • Ask what happens if the problem comes back, and make sure anything promised is on the estimate.

What to do right now if you suspect a slab leak

  1. Turn off all water and check the meter for movement.
  2. If the meter keeps moving, shut off the main water valve to stop the damage.
  3. Note warm spots, damp areas and sounds, and take photos.
  4. Move rugs and furniture off any warm or damp flooring.
  5. Call us. Do not break concrete yourself.

Slab Leak Repair FAQs

How do I know if a pipe is leaking under my foundation?

Signs include an unexplained high water bill, a meter that moves with everything off, warm or wet floor spots, the sound of running water, low pressure, and musty smells or mold near walls. A leak detection visit confirms it and locates the leak.

Can a slab leak be repaired without major demolition?

Often yes. Rerouting a water supply line through the attic or walls can avoid breaking the slab, and tunneling from outside can reach the pipe without tearing up indoor floors. The right option depends on your home and the type of line.

What causes slab leaks?

Shifting clay soil, corrosion, abrasion against concrete or gravel, aging pipe and fittings, high water pressure and installation problems. Hot water lines often fail first.

Will a slab leak damage my foundation?

A long-running leak can soften soil under the slab and contribute to movement, flooring damage and mold. Fixing it early limits the damage. If you see widening cracks or sloping floors, consider a structural opinion.

Will insurance pay for a slab leak?

It depends on your policy and the cause. Some policies cover resulting damage but not the pipe, and wear and tear is often excluded. Check with your insurer and keep written findings and receipts.

Will I know the price before work starts?

Yes. You get a written estimate listing labor, parts and any trip or diagnostic fee, and we wait for your approval. If we find something that changes the scope, we stop and talk to you first.
