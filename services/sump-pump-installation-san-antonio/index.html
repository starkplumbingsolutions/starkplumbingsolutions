Quick answer

A sump pump collects drainage water in a pit and pumps it away from the property. San Antonio properties that need one include low lots, crawl spaces, drainage pits and areas where storm runoff collects. Pumps fail from clogs, stuck floats, power loss and missing check valves, so battery backup is worth considering.

When a Property Needs a Sump Pump

Most of Bexar County is not basement country, so sump pumps here mainly serve crawl spaces, low yards, drainage pits and properties with persistent groundwater or runoff. If water collects in one area and cannot flow away by gravity, a pump is the answer.

  • Water in crawl space after storms
  • A low spot where gravity drainage isn't possible
  • A French drain with no gravity outlet
  • Moisture, mold or musty smell below the home

Sump Pump Installation

We build or upgrade the pit, set the pump level on a stable base, install a check valve, run discharge piping and test the float under load. Wiring should be on a dedicated GFCI-protected circuit installed by a licensed electrician where required.

Submersible and Pedestal Pump Systems

Submersible pumps sit in the pit. They run quieter and handle most residential needs. Pedestal pumps have the motor above the pit; they are easier to service but louder and more exposed. For most San Antonio homes we recommend submersible.

Sump Pump Repair and Replacement

Common failures include a stuck or tangled float, clogged intake, burned motor from running dry or short cycling, and a missing check valve that lets water drain back into the pit. Pumps typically last about seven to ten years. If yours is older and cycling hard, replace it before the next storm.

Battery Backup Pump Options

Storms cause power loss when you need the pump most. A battery backup keeps a second pump running. It needs a battery you maintain and replace on schedule.

Discharge Pipe Installation

Discharge must go away from the foundation to a lawful outlet: a lower area, emitter or curb where permitted. Not into a sanitary sewer or onto a neighbor's property. Insulate or slope the pipe to avoid trapped water and freeze damage.

This is about moving drainage water, not boosting water pressure. If you have low pressure inside the home, see our water booster pump service.

Don't DIY this

  • Run discharge into the sewer line
  • Skip the check valve
  • Plug a sump pump into an extension cord
  • Ignore a pump that runs nonstop
  • Store items on top of the pit cover

FAQs

What causes a sump pump to fail?

Stuck floats, clogged intakes, worn motors, power outages and missing check valves are the main causes.

Should a sump pump have a backup system?

In storm-prone areas, yes. A battery backup keeps the system running during power loss.

Where should sump pump discharge water go?

Away from the foundation to a lawful outlet like a lower area, emitter or curb where permitted. Not to the sanitary sewer.

How long do sump pumps last?

Typically around seven to ten years, depending on use.

Is a sump pump the same as a booster pump?

No. A sump pump removes drainage water. A booster pump raises supply pressure.
