When a Booster Pump Is Needed

A booster makes sense when measured pressure at the home is consistently low, the supply is adequate, and the plumbing is sound. It does not fix leaks, clogged aerators or a bad regulator.

  • Static pressure below roughly 40 psi
  • Supply flow is available
  • No leaks or restrictions found
  • Home is on a long or elevated run

Diagnosing Low Pressure First

We install gauges at the hose bib, test with fixtures open and closed, check the regulator, main valve, filters and pipe material, then decide. Sometimes the fix is a regulator or a section of pipe, not a pump.

Booster Pump Types and Sizing

Single-speed pumps are simpler. Variable-speed constant-pressure systems hold steady pressure and are quieter. Sizing depends on fixtures, flow demand and the pump's limits. Oversizing can cause noise and pipe stress.

Installation Details That Matter

A proper install includes a bypass so water still flows if the pump fails, check valves, isolation valves, gauges on both sides, correct electrical protection and a pressure relief path. Cheap installs skip these.

Repair and Replacement

Booster pumps fail from run-dry events, worn seals and bad controls. We test, repair what makes sense and replace what does not.

How a Booster Pump Differs From a Well Pump

A well pump lifts water from the ground. A booster raises pressure in a line that already has water.

Please don’t DIY this

  • Do not buy a pump before measuring pressure.
  • Do not push pressure above safe limits: appliances and pipes can fail.
  • Do not wire pumps yourself without correct protection.
  • Do not install without a bypass and check valves.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will a booster pump fix low water pressure?

Only if supply is adequate and the plumbing is sound. We test first.

Should pressure problems be diagnosed before installing a pump?

Yes. Many low-pressure cases are caused by regulators, leaks or clogged pipe.

How is a booster pump different from a well pump?

A well pump brings water out of the ground. A booster increases pressure for water already in your pipes.

Can a booster damage my plumbing?

An oversized or badly set pump can. Correct sizing and relief protection prevent that.
