Common Kitchen Sink Drain Problems

Kitchen drains work harder than any other drain in the house. Cooking grease, food scraps, coffee grounds and soap all go down them, and they cool and stick as they travel.

  • Slow draining or standing water.
  • Gurgling when the dishwasher runs.
  • Bad odors from the drain.
  • Water backing up into the second basin or the dishwasher.

Food Grease and Soap Buildup

Grease is the number one cause of kitchen clogs. Hot grease runs down easily, then cools and hardens on the pipe wall. Each wash layers more soap and food on top until the pipe is a fraction of its original size.

Hot water and dish soap do not remove hardened grease. Neither does a snake that only pokes a hole through it. Removing it takes cleaning the pipe wall, which is where Hydro Jetting comes in for stubborn cases.

Hard water adds mineral scale on top of the grease in many San Antonio homes, which is another reason clogs recur.

Clogs in P-Traps and Drain Branches

The curved P-trap under the sink holds a bit of water to block sewer gas, and it is also where food and grease collect first. Beyond it, the horizontal drain branch runs into the wall where it joins the main drain.

We clear the trap and the branch, then test the flow. If water still moves slowly after the branch is clear, the clog is further down or the line has a sag, and we explain what that means.

Preventing Recurring Kitchen Clogs

  • Wipe greasy pans with a paper towel and throw it in the trash.
  • Do not pour fats, oils or coffee grounds down the drain.
  • Run hot water for 30 seconds after using the disposal.
  • Use a sink strainer for scraps.
  • Do not rely on chemical drain openers. They can damage pipes and make a later repair more hazardous.

If clogs keep coming back, ask about Kitchen Plumbing Repair to check venting, slope and the branch line.

What to do right now if your kitchen sink is clogged

  1. Stop running water and stop using the dishwasher.
  2. Do not run the garbage disposal if the sink is full of standing water.
  3. Place a bucket under the P-trap, then unscrew it and clear it if you are comfortable doing so.
  4. Skip chemical drain openers. They rarely clear grease and they make later work riskier.
  5. If the other sink or the dishwasher shows water, call us.
Never put your hand into a disposal. Turn off the power at the breaker before you work near it.

Kitchen Sink Drain Cleaning FAQs

Why does my kitchen sink drain slowly?

Usually because grease, food scraps and soap have built up on the inside of the trap or drain branch, narrowing it. Hard-water scale can add to the buildup. Slow draining almost always becomes a full clog if ignored.

Why does water back up into the other sink basin?

The clog is below the point where the two basins join, so water has nowhere to go but up the other side. A plunger rarely fixes it. The branch usually needs to be cleared past the junction.

Can a garbage disposal cause a kitchen drain clog?

Yes. A disposal grinds food into a slurry that can settle in the line, and a jammed or failing disposal can restrict flow. If the disposal hums or the clog returns soon after clearing, see Garbage Disposal Repair & Installation.

Do baking soda and vinegar actually unclog a kitchen drain?

They can reduce light odors, but they do not clear a real grease clog. Heavy buildup needs mechanical clearing, and stacking drain chemicals can be unsafe.
