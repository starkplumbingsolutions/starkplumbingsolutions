Signs Your Home May Need Repiping

Repiping is worth considering when leaks keep returning in different places, copper shows pinhole leaks, water runs rusty or discolored, pressure is weak at every fixture, or the home has galvanized steel or gray polybutylene pipe. Homes more than 30 to 40 years old with original plumbing often reach this stage. One leak is usually a repair. Several leaks in a few years usually point to the system.

Causes of Recurring Pipe Leaks

Scale and corrosion thin pipe walls from the inside, especially where water is hard. Water chemistry, pressure swings, freezing and poor original installation also play a role. Fittings and joints are common failure points. When several of these appear together, patching one leak at a time often costs more than replacing the supply system.

PEX vs. Copper Repiping

PEX is flexible tubing with fewer fittings and fewer wall openings, and it resists scale and freezing better than copper in many conditions. Copper is rigid, metal and long established, and holds up well when installed correctly. Both are used in San Antonio homes. We recommend the material based on your layout, budget and water quality, and explain what each choice means for future repairs.

The Whole-House Repiping Process

We begin with a survey of the supply system, mapping routes and fixture counts, then deliver a written plan and estimate. Work proceeds in stages so water is restored at the end of each day when possible. After installation we pressure test the system before walls are closed. Many whole-home repipes finish in a few days, depending on home size and access.

Replacing Old Supply Pipes

Replacement focuses on the supply side that feeds your fixtures. We coordinate wall and ceiling access and include patching details in the estimate, so you know what to expect for drywall and paint. Drain lines are assessed separately because they follow different rules.

Repiping During a Renovation

The most cost-effective time to repipe is during a remodel, when walls, ceilings and floors are already open. Bathroom and kitchen remodels are good windows. Tell us about planned work and we can schedule the pipe work with the rest of the project.

Repiping vs. Repairing Individual Leaks

A single failed joint in an otherwise sound system is a repair. Repiping makes sense when leaks keep appearing, the material is failing across the home, or you are planning to sell and want to avoid surprises in an inspection.

Don't DIY repiping or cut into walls. Hidden pipes sit beside wiring, gas lines and framing, and cutting in the wrong place causes costly damage. PEX fittings and crimps must be installed with the correct tools and technique. A single bad connection can leak behind a wall for months before anyone sees it.

Seasonal Issues in San Antonio

  • Winter: exposed attic and exterior runs can burst in a hard freeze. Repiping in spring avoids rushed winter repairs.
  • Summer: high pressure during heavy use can expose weak joints. Watch for drips in hot months.
  • Fall: a good season to schedule a planning inspection before the winter and holiday rush.
  • Year-round: a gradually rising water bill can signal leaks inside the walls. Track it monthly.

Before the Plumber Arrives: Checklist

Having these ready shortens the visit and helps us quote accurately.

  • Write down the year the house was built and any past plumbing work
  • Count bathrooms, kitchens and laundry connections
  • Record each leak with its location and date
  • Note whether the water runs brown or cloudy in the morning
  • Photograph the pipes at the water heater and under sinks
  • Tell us about any planned remodel

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my house needs repiping?

Repeated leaks in different places, rusty or discolored water, weak pressure throughout the home and older pipe materials are the main signs. A pressure and visual inspection gives a clear answer.

Is PEX suitable for repiping?

For most residential supply systems, yes. PEX is flexible, resists scale and freezing better than copper in many cases, and uses fewer fittings. We confirm the right material for your system, code and water conditions.

What is the difference between repiping and repairing individual leaks?

A repair fixes one failure. Repiping replaces the supply system across the home, which makes sense when failures are widespread or keep recurring.

How long does a whole-house repipe take?

Many homes are completed in a few days, depending on size, fixture count and access. We provide a schedule with the estimate.
