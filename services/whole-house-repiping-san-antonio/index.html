Signs Your Home May Need Repiping

Repiping is worth considering when leaks keep returning in different places, copper shows pinhole leaks, water runs rusty or discolored, pressure is weak at every fixture, or the home has galvanized steel or gray polybutylene pipe. Homes more than 30 to 40 years old with original plumbing often reach this stage. One leak is usually a repair. Several leaks in a few years usually point to the system.

♨️

Causes of Recurring Pipe Leaks

Scale and corrosion thin pipe walls from the inside, especially where water is hard. Water chemistry, pressure swings, freezing and poor original installation also play a role. Fittings and joints are common failure points. When several of these appear together, patching one leak at a time often costs more than replacing the supply system.

💧

PEX vs. Copper Repiping

PEX is flexible tubing with fewer fittings and fewer wall openings, and it resists scale and freezing better than copper in many conditions. Copper is rigid, metal and long established, and holds up well when installed correctly. Both are used in San Antonio homes. We recommend the material based on your layout, budget and water quality, and explain what each choice means for future repairs.

🔥

The Whole-House Repiping Process

We begin with a survey of the supply system, mapping routes and fixture counts, then deliver a written plan and estimate. Work proceeds in stages so water is restored at the end of each day when possible. After installation we pressure test the system before walls are closed. Many whole-home repipes finish in a few days, depending on home size and access.

🧪

Replacing Old Supply Pipes

Replacement focuses on the supply side that feeds your fixtures. We coordinate wall and ceiling access and include patching details in the estimate, so you know what to expect for drywall and paint. Drain lines are assessed separately because they follow different rules.

⚖️

Repiping During a Renovation

The most cost-effective time to repipe is during a remodel, when walls, ceilings and floors are already open. Bathroom and kitchen remodels are good windows. Tell us about planned work and we can schedule the pipe work with the rest of the project.

🛠️

Repiping vs. Repairing Individual Leaks

A single failed joint in an otherwise sound system is a repair. Repiping makes sense when leaks keep appearing, the material is failing across the home, or you are planning to sell and want to avoid surprises in an inspection.

📋

Pipe Materials Found in San Antonio Homes

Pipe type tells a lot about repipe risk. Galvanized steel in older homes rusts from the inside and narrows over time. Copper is long-lived but can develop pinholes. Gray polybutylene, used in many homes built decades ago, is widely considered a failure risk and a common reason for repiping. CPVC is common in homes from the 1990s onward, can become brittle with age and heat, and some buyers and inspectors flag it. PEX is the modern flexible option. We identify what you have and explain what it means for your plan.

🧰

One-Story Slab Homes vs. Two-Story Homes

In slab-on-grade homes, supply lines often run through the attic and down walls, which makes repiping through the attic practical without breaking the slab. Two-story homes add vertical runs and more wall openings. Homes with lines under the slab need a different conversation, such as rerouting overhead rather than opening concrete. We survey your layout and propose the least invasive route.

💡

What Happens to Drywall, Paint and Your Day

Repiping needs access. Expect small openings in drywall at fixtures and sometimes in ceilings or closets. We plan access points carefully, protect floors and furniture, and discuss patching in the estimate so there is no surprise. Water is usually off for part of each day, so we schedule around cooking, showers and work-from-home needs. Most homeowners reported being surprised by how organized the process was once the schedule was explained.

🏠

Partial Repipe or Whole-House Repipe?

If only one branch is failing, a partial repipe can solve the problem for less. If most of the system is the same age and material, replacing part of it often leads to the next failure a few feet away. Hold on to the leak history you have: dates, locations and past repair invoices. That record is the best evidence for choosing between a partial and a whole-house repipe.

🔧

Insurance, Claims and Leak Documentation

Coverage varies widely. Many policies cover sudden water damage but not the pipe itself, and some exclude certain pipe materials or repeated leaks. Ask your insurer what is covered before you sign a repiping contract, and keep photos and reports. Our written inspection report helps if you pursue a claim. Some homeowners with recurring leaks from defective products have pursued legal or manufacturer remedies, so preserving failed pipe samples can matter. We can set failed sections aside when we remove them.

🔍

Buying or Selling a Home With Old Pipes

Buyers sometimes learn from an inspection that a house has polybutylene, CPVC or aging galvanized pipe and ask what to do. A plumber's inspection and written estimate give both sides facts to negotiate. Sellers can reduce surprises by getting an inspection before listing. We provide a written scope and price that agents and lenders can review.

♨️

Cost Factors and Comparing Quotes

The price depends on home size, fixture count, stories, access, material, how much wall repair is included and the permit. Compare quotes line by line: what material, how many fixtures, whether drywall and paint repair are included, what the warranty covers and in writing who handles permits. A lower price that excludes patching can cost more in the end. Ask every contractor, including us, for the license number and proof of insurance.

💧

Water Quality, Hard Water and Pipe Life

San Antonio water is hard, and water chemistry affects pipe life. Scale narrows pipes and stresses fittings. Pressure that is too high stresses joints. We check pressure during an inspection, and a regulator or softener recommendation is part of a good repipe plan, so the new system lasts.

🔥

Preparing Your Home for a Repipe

Plan for noise, dust and short water outages. Clear closets, vanity areas and attic access so crews can reach the pipes. Move wall hangings and fragile items from walls that will be opened. Cover or relocate valuables, and arrange for pets. Tell us about work-from-home needs, babies, medical needs and daily routines so we can plan the shutoff windows. We protect floors and furniture, and we discuss patch and paint expectations in writing before work begins.

🧪

After the Repipe: Warranty, Flushing and Pressure

After installation, expect some air and cloudy water at first, and flush faucets as we direct. Check fixtures for drips over the first few days and report anything. Keep the written scope, the permit record and the warranty information with your home records, because they matter when you sell or file a claim. Ask about pressure regulators and a water treatment plan to protect the new system from the same conditions that wore out the old one.

⚖️

Why Repipe Before the Next Leak

Failing pipe rarely stays quiet. The first leak is often small and hidden, and by the time a stain appears, the framing, insulation and flooring may be wet. Planning a repipe lets you choose the timing, the material and the contractor, instead of rushing a decision during a flood. It also lets you coordinate with other projects, such as remodeling or painting, and prevents repeat repairs that add up. If your home has the signs listed above, ask for an inspection before the next failure forces your hand.

🛠️

A Typical Repiping Timeline, Day by Day

Every home is different, but a typical sequence looks like this. Before day one, we inspect, deliver the written plan and set the date. On day one, we protect floors, shut off the water, map the new routes and begin opening access points and running new lines to the fixtures. On day two, we connect fixtures, install shutoff valves, make the tie-in to the main and restore water in stages. Before closing walls, we pressure test the system and look for leaks. Then we remove the old supply lines where practical, patch the access points as agreed and clean up. Larger or two-story homes can take longer, and we give you the schedule before you commit.

Throughout the process we explain each change, and we call you before any change in the plan or price. You should never be surprised by a bill at the end.

For households with young children, older family members or work-from-home needs, tell us up front. We can stage the work so one bathroom and the kitchen have water as much as possible during the day.

📋

Repiping a Rental, Duplex or Small Multifamily Property

Owners of older rentals often face repeated leak calls, tenant complaints and rising water bills. A repipe can stop the cycle, but it needs coordination. We schedule around tenants, provide advance notice templates, work in phases when needed and keep a clear record of each unit. A written scope and price per unit helps with budgeting and with lender or insurer requirements. If you manage several properties, tell us your portfolio and we can plan a sequence that prioritizes the worst leak histories first.

🧰

Questions to Settle Before You Sign a Repipe Contract

Confirm the material, the fixture count, the wall repair scope, the permit, the schedule and the warranty in writing. Ask who is on site each day and who you call with questions. Compare quotes on the same scope, not just the final number. A clear contract protects both sides and keeps the work on track.

If anything in a proposal is unclear, ask us to explain it. We would rather answer ten questions now than leave you with doubts later.

Before the Plumber Arrives

Having these ready shortens the visit and helps us quote accurately.

  • Write down the year the house was built and any past plumbing work
  • Count bathrooms, kitchens and laundry connections
  • Record each leak with its location and date
  • Note whether the water runs brown or cloudy in the morning
  • Photograph the pipes at the water heater and under sinks
  • Tell us about any planned remodel

Seasonal Issues in San Antonio

  • Winter: exposed attic and exterior runs can burst in a hard freeze. Repiping in spring avoids rushed winter repairs.
  • Summer: high pressure during heavy use can expose weak joints. Watch for drips in hot months.
  • Fall: a good season to schedule a planning inspection before the winter and holiday rush.
  • Year-round: a gradually rising water bill can signal leaks inside the walls. Track it monthly.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Waiting for a major flood before replacing failing pipe
  • Comparing quotes without confirming wall repair is included
  • Repairing leak after leak without tracking where they occur
  • Ignoring brown or discolored water that returns after flushing
  • Skipping a pressure check before and after the repipe
  • Throwing away failed pipe pieces you might need as evidence

Plain-English Glossary

PEX

Polybutylene

CPVC

Rigid plastic pipe used in many newer homes.

Galvanized

Steel pipe coated in zinc that corrodes inside.

Manifold

Pressure test

A test that checks the system for leaks before closing walls.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my house needs repiping?

Repeated leaks in different places, rusty or discolored water, weak pressure throughout the home and older pipe materials are the main signs. A pressure and visual inspection gives a clear answer.

Is PEX suitable for repiping?

For most residential supply systems, yes. PEX is flexible, resists scale and freezing better than copper in many cases, and uses fewer fittings. We confirm the right material for your system, code and water conditions.

What is the difference between repiping and repairing individual leaks?

A repair fixes one failure. Repiping replaces the supply system across the home, which makes sense when failures are widespread or keep recurring.

How long does a whole-house repipe take?

Many homes are completed in a few days, depending on size, fixture count and access. We provide a schedule with the estimate.

How much does it cost to repipe a house in San Antonio?

It varies with square footage, fixtures, stories, access and finish repair. We provide a written estimate after an inspection so you can compare quotes on the same scope.

Can you repipe a house without removing drywall?

Some access can be gained through attics, crawlspaces and closets, but most repipes need some small wall openings. We explain where and how they will be patched.

Will insurance cover repiping?

Sometimes for resulting water damage, rarely for the pipe itself. Check your policy and ask your insurer before work.

Is PEX safe for drinking water?

PEX is approved for potable water systems in building codes when installed correctly with the right fittings. We follow code and manufacturer instructions.
