How Tankless Water Heaters Work

A tankless unit heats water only when a hot tap or shower opens. A flow sensor triggers a gas burner or electric element, so you get continuous hot water and skip the standby heat loss of a storage tank. Performance depends on flow rate in gallons per minute (GPM) and the temperature rise your home needs, which is why sizing matters.

Tankless Water Heater Installation

Gas units need an adequately sized gas line, proper venting (direct vent or condensing, depending on the model) and a condensate drain for condensing models. Electric units draw heavy current. Many whole-home models need a dedicated high-amperage circuit, and some need more than one. Mounting location, clearances and a water-quality plan all shape the install.

Tankless Water Heater Repairs

Error codes often point to a flow sensor, ignition or igniter, a scaled heat exchanger, a venting problem or a failed temperature sensor. Resetting the unit over and over without finding the cause can hide a serious fault. We read the code, test the parts and explain what it means before recommending a fix.

Sizing for Household Hot Water Demand

An undersized tankless unit gives lukewarm water when two showers run at once. An oversized one costs more than it needs to. We size by the number of fixtures used at the same time and the flow each one needs, then match the unit to your gas or electric supply.

Scale Buildup and Descaling

San Antonio water is hard, and tankless heat exchangers are sensitive to scale. Mineral buildup reduces flow and efficiency and can cause overheating. Manufacturers typically call for periodic descaling, and many warranties depend on proper water treatment. A whole-home softener or filtration system can protect the unit. We can assess your water first.

Tank vs. Tankless: Which Fits Your Home?

A tank suits homes that want lower upfront cost and simple service. A tankless unit suits homes that want continuous hot water, have space for wall mounting and will keep up with maintenance. Either can be the right answer, and we will show you the difference using your own numbers.

Don't DIY tankless descaling or repairs. Acid descaling without the manufacturer's procedure can damage seals and parts. Gas and electrical work on tankless units is code regulated, and a venting fault can cause carbon monoxide. Use a licensed plumber for every step that touches gas, power or the heat exchanger.

Seasonal Issues in San Antonio

  • Spring and summer: scale builds fastest with heavy use. Schedule descaling before peak months.
  • Fall: inspect venting for debris, nests and wear before cold weather.
  • Winter: demand spikes during cold showers and holidays. Do not ignore intermittent error codes.
  • After storms or outages: power surges can damage control boards. Have the unit checked after a major event.

Before the Plumber Arrives: Checklist

Having these ready shortens the visit and helps us quote accurately.

  • Write down the exact error code and when it appears
  • Note the model and serial number on the label
  • Record whether you have a water softener and its condition
  • Count the fixtures you use at the same time
  • Photograph the venting and any drips or stains
  • Note the last descale or service date, if you know it

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a tankless system replace a traditional tank heater?

Yes, in many homes. The swap depends on gas line capacity, venting, electrical service and available space. We check those first so the unit is sized and installed properly.

Why is my tankless water heater showing an error code?

Codes usually indicate a sensor, ignition, flow, scale or venting issue. Avoid repeated resets. Call a technician to read the code and test the cause.

Does hard water affect tankless systems?

Yes. Scale on the heat exchanger reduces flow and efficiency and can cause overheating. Descaling on schedule and proper water treatment help protect the unit and its warranty.

How much does a tankless installation cost in San Antonio?

Published market ranges commonly run from about $5,000 to $7,000 depending on model, fuel and layout. Your written estimate covers your exact situation.
