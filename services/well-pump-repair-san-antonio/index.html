Signs of a Failing Well Pump

Most well pumps give warnings before they quit: spitting faucets, cloudy or sandy water, a higher electric bill, a pump that cycles every few seconds, or pressure that will not build. In the San Antonio area, many private wells draw from the Edwards and Trinity aquifers, and falling water levels in dry summers can look like pump failure when the pump is fine. We separate the two before recommending anything.

  • Pressure gauge never reaches cut-off
  • Clicking at the pressure switch
  • Water takes minutes to return after heavy use
  • Burning smell or hot motor at the wellhead

How We Diagnose a Well System

We start with the simplest, cheapest checks and move down the list: power at the panel and disconnect, the pressure switch contacts, tank pre-charge, the gauge, and then amp draw on the motor. Amp draw tells us whether a submersible motor is healthy, overloaded or dead without pulling the pump. If the pump must be pulled, we explain why first.

  • Voltage and amp-draw test
  • Pressure tank pre-charge check
  • Pressure switch and cut-in/cut-out settings
  • Check valve and drop pipe review
  • Flow and recovery test

Submersible and Jet Pump Repairs

Submersible pumps sit in the casing and are pulled for service; jet pumps sit above ground and are easier to reach. Repairs range from replacing a capacitor, control box or switch to replacing the pump, pitless adapter, wire or drop pipe. We tell you honestly when repair no longer makes sense, such as an older pump with a failed motor and corroded fittings.

Pressure Switches, Tanks and Controls

A bad pressure switch or waterlogged tank causes many calls that look like pump failures. Replacing a switch or restoring tank charge costs far less than a pump, and a healthy tank protects the new pump. Constant-pressure controllers can also smooth out the surging many homeowners complain about.

Please don’t DIY this

  • Do not keep resetting a tripped breaker: the motor may be shorted.
  • Do not open the pressure tank or switch with power on. Well systems run on 240 volts.
  • Do not pour bleach or chemicals down the well to fix pressure.
  • Do not pull a submersible pump yourself: wet wiring, weight and open casings cause injuries.

Seasonal Issues in San Antonio

Summer drought

Lower water tables and heavy irrigation demand make marginal pumps fail first. Watch for sputtering after lawn watering.

Winter freeze

Exposed wellheads, pressure tanks and switch lines can freeze. Insulate and keep a heat source on the pump house.

Storm season

Power surges and flooding damage control boxes and motors. A surge protector at the panel is cheap insurance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why did my well suddenly stop producing water?

Common causes are a tripped breaker, failed pressure switch, burned-out motor, broken drop pipe or a lowered water level. A power and pressure test usually narrows it down quickly.

How can I tell whether the well pump or pressure tank has failed?

A pump that runs but builds no pressure points toward the pump or drop pipe. Rapid clicking on and off with normal water points toward a waterlogged tank or bad switch.

Can a submersible well pump be replaced?

Yes. The pump is pulled from the casing, inspected with the wire and drop pipe, and a correctly sized pump is installed. Replacement is common after 10 to 15 years of service.
