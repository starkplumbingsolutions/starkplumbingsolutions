How Sewer Camera Inspections Work

A small waterproof camera on a flexible push cable is fed through a cleanout or other access point and moves through the line while a screen shows what it sees. A locator can mark the depth and position of the camera on the surface, so any repair can target the exact spot.

A cleanout makes the process faster and cleaner. If your home does not have one, the camera can sometimes go in through a toilet or vent, but that can limit what it can see, and some inspections end up incomplete. Ask about adding a cleanout.

When to Inspect a Sewer Line

  • Before buying a home, especially an older one.
  • When drains are slow, gurgling or backing up.
  • When clogs return after cleaning.
  • When you see wet ground, sinkholes or smell sewage outside.
  • Before a major remodel, landscaping or tree removal near the line.
  • After a repair, to confirm the work.

Detecting Root Intrusion and Pipe Damage

San Antonio's expansive clay soil and mature trees are hard on sewer lines. The camera shows the problems that matter.

  • Roots entering at joints or cracks.
  • Cracks and breaks in clay, cast iron or plastic pipe.
  • Separated joints where soil has moved.
  • Bellies or sags that hold water and waste.
  • Corrosion and scale in older cast iron.
  • Collapsed or crushed pipe.

Inspection for Recurring Blockages

If you have paid to have the same clog cleared more than once, the camera is how you stop paying for it. It shows whether the cause is buildup that needs Hydro Jetting, roots, or a structural problem that needs Sewer Line Repair.

A tip from plumbers: a camera in a line that is completely full of debris shows nothing. If the line is blocked, we usually clear it first, then inspect.

Sewer Scopes Before Home Purchases

Standard home inspections generally do not cover the sewer line, and home inspectors typically point you to a plumber for a quote on anything they cannot see. On forums, first-time buyers repeatedly say the same thing: get the sewer scope before closing.

A pre-purchase scope can find root damage, cracks and collapse before you own the problem, and give you facts to negotiate repairs or credit. Ask for the video and a written summary.

What Does a Sewer Camera Inspection Cost in San Antonio?

Standalone sewer camera inspections typically run about $150 to $500, with some sources citing $175 to $400.

The price depends on access, line length and whether you want a recording and a line locate. Some shops advertise a free camera look with a paid repair, which can be fine, but ask what the free look includes, and whether you get to see the footage.

What moves the price

  • Access: an existing cleanout is faster and cheaper.
  • Line length and complexity.
  • Whether you need video recording and a written report.
  • Whether the line must be cleared first so the camera can pass.

How to avoid overpaying

  • Ask whether the price includes a recording and a written summary you can keep.
  • Ask for the price in writing before work starts, with labor, parts and any trip or diagnostic fee listed separately.
  • Ask the plumber to explain the cause in plain words. If you get a vague answer, get a second opinion.
  • Compare what is included, not just the headline price. A cheap special that grows into a long add-on list is not cheap.
  • Never let scope change on the fly. If something new is found, work should stop until you approve it.
  • Ask what happens if the problem comes back, and make sure anything promised is on the estimate.

What to do right now before a camera inspection

  1. Find your cleanout, usually a capped pipe in the yard near the house or by the foundation.
  2. Clear the area around it so the technician can reach it.
  3. Note which fixtures are slow and when the symptoms started.
  4. If you are buying a home, ask for access to the cleanout from the seller or agent.
  5. Do not pour chemical drain openers into a line you plan to inspect.

Sewer Camera Inspection FAQs

What can a sewer camera detect?

Roots, cracks, separated joints, bellies, corrosion, scale, grease, collapsed sections and foreign objects. It also shows whether a line is clear and sound.

When should I get a sewer scope?

Before buying a home, when drains are slow or backing up, when clogs keep returning, when you see wet ground or smell sewage outside, and before major work near the line.

Can the camera locate the cause of recurring clogs?

Yes. It shows whether buildup, roots, a sag or a crack is causing the problem, so you fix the cause instead of paying to clear the same clog again.

Why do I need a clean line before the camera?

A camera in a line full of debris shows nothing. If the line is blocked, we usually clear it first, then inspect.

What if my house has no cleanout?

A technician may be able to enter through a toilet or vent, but access is limited and the inspection may be incomplete. Adding a cleanout makes this and future work far easier.

Will I know the price before work starts?

Yes. You get a written estimate listing labor, parts and any trip or diagnostic fee, and we wait for your approval. If we find something that changes the scope, we stop and talk to you first.
