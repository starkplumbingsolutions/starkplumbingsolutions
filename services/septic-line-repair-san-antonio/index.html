Signs of a Damaged Septic Line

Slow drains throughout the house, gurgling, sewage odors, wet spots between the home and tank, and repeat clogs all point to the line. Vehicles driving over a shallow line, shifting San Antonio clay soil and tree roots are common causes.

Line Clog vs. Full Tank

A clogged line stops flow before it reaches the tank. A full tank backs up from the tank side. The two look similar but need different fixes, and pumping alone will not clear a broken pipe.

Camera Inspection

A camera shows roots, bellies, cracks, offsets and blockages, and a locator marks the depth and location. That means repairs are targeted, not guesswork.

  • Locates breaks and offsets
  • Shows roots and grease
  • Confirms line slope
  • Documents condition for you

Repair vs. Replacement

Spot repairs fit isolated breaks. Replacement fits long runs of collapsed or sagging pipe. Cost depends on length, depth, access and what the line crosses, such as concrete or driveways. You get both options in writing.

Cleanouts and Connections

A proper cleanout makes future service easy. We can add or repair cleanouts and fix connections at the house and tank.

Please don’t DIY this

  • Do not use chemical drain cleaners: they harm septic bacteria and old pipe.
  • Do not flush wipes, grease or feminine products.
  • Do not drive heavy vehicles over a shallow line.
  • Do not enter a septic tank: gases can be fatal.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if the pipe to my septic tank is clogged?

If many drains slow at once while the tank is not full, the line is a likely cause.

Can roots damage septic sewer lines?

Yes. Roots enter joints and cracks and can block or break the pipe.

Is a septic line backup the same as a full septic tank?

No. They have different causes. A camera inspection and tank check tell them apart.

Do I need a septic license for line work?

Some onsite sewage work needs authorized contractors. We explain what applies before starting.
