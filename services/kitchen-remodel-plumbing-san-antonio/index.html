Plumbing for Kitchen Remodeling

A kitchen remodel changes where water comes in and where it goes out. New cabinets, an island, a bigger sink, a pot filler or a new dishwasher location all change plumbing. Done well, the room feels effortless. Done poorly, you get slow drains, gurgling, leaks under the sink and cabinets that must be torn out to reach a pipe.

Local homeowners ask the same questions again and again: can the sink go on the island, how much does it cost, do I need a permit, and who do I call first? They also share cautionary tales, such as hidden water damage found behind a previous kitchen remodel, simple faucet jobs that turned into multi-hour billable “escapades,” and rubber hoses between the drain and disposal that make everyone nervous.

This page covers layout changes and new connections during kitchen renovations. For repairs to an existing kitchen, see kitchen plumbing repair.

What kitchen remodel plumbing includes

  • Removing and capping old supply and drain lines
  • Relocating the sink, dishwasher and disposal
  • Adding island sinks, prep sinks or pot fillers
  • New hot and cold supply lines and shutoffs
  • New drains, traps and vents
  • Refrigerator and ice-maker water lines
  • Gas line coordination for range or cooktop changes
  • Final fixture hookup and function testing
Best timing: Call your plumber when cabinet layout is drawn, before cabinets are ordered and before demolition covers up what is in the walls.

Kitchen Sink and Island Relocation

Can a kitchen sink be moved to an island? Yes, but it is one of the more involved remodel plumbing jobs because an island has no wall to hide pipes in.

What an island sink needs

  • Supply lines run up through the floor and into the cabinet.
  • A drain that slopes to the main line, often through the floor.
  • A vent. Without one, the drain gurgles and may siphon. Because pipes cannot rise through the island's open sides, an island often uses a code-approved loop vent or air-admittance solution, depending on local rules and inspector acceptance.
  • Slab or crawlspace access. On a slab, we saw-cut the concrete, trench, install pipe and patch. On a raised foundation, we run lines under the floor.

Don’t DIY this. Cutting a slab or routing a vent through cabinetry without a plan can damage post-tension cables, structural elements or other utilities. Do not cut or core a slab without a licensed plumber and proper locating.

New Water Supply Lines

A remodel is the right time to upgrade old supply lines. Many older San Antonio homes have galvanized steel that rusts from the inside, narrowing flow and causing low pressure at the kitchen faucet. Others have aging copper or early plastics that are due for replacement.

What we check and replace

  • Shutoff valves under the sink, which often seize or leak
  • Supply lines to faucet, dishwasher and ice-maker
  • Pressure at the kitchen sink, to help diagnose weak flow
  • Pipe material and condition inside the wall
  • Water hammer, caused by quick-closing valves and unsecured pipe

Add-ons worth planning now

  • Pot filler: Needs a dedicated cold line, shutoff and blocking in the wall
  • Reverse osmosis: Under-sink drinking water filtration. See reverse osmosis installation.
  • Instant hot water or a recirculation loop: Reduces the wait for hot water
  • Water softener tie-in: San Antonio water is hard, and it scales faucets, dishwashers and ice-makers

Plan for hot-water distance too. If your water heater is far from the new kitchen layout, wait time grows. We can discuss shorter runs, insulation and recirculation.

Drain Rerouting and Venting

Kitchen drains clog more than any other drain in the house, mostly because of grease, food waste and soap residue. Poor slope or too many turns makes it worse.

Why venting matters

Venting lets air into the drain so water flows freely and traps keep their seal. Without it you get slow drains, gurgling and sewer odor. When a sink moves, the vent usually moves too.

Good practices we follow

  • Correct pipe size and slope for the new sink location
  • Long-sweep fittings and minimal elbows
  • Accessible cleanouts, ideally near the kitchen branch
  • Dishwasher drain connected properly, with a high loop or air gap as required
  • Disposal connected with rigid or approved components, with no sagging hoses that trap debris

Grease, the silent drain killer

Even a perfect new drain will clog if grease gets poured down it. Wipe pans, use strainers and schedule periodic cleaning if you cook often. See kitchen sink drain cleaning for existing problem lines.

If old cast iron drains in your remodel are scaled or cracked, replacing sections now is cheaper than cutting into your new floors later.

Dishwasher and Refrigerator Connections

Can a dishwasher be relocated? Yes. It needs a hot water supply stop, a drain connection (with a high loop or air gap) and an electrical outlet, and it must fit the cabinet opening. Moving it usually means new supply and drain routing, and sometimes coordination with the electrician.

Dishwasher

  • Hot water supply line with an accessible shutoff
  • Drain connected to the disposal or sink tailpiece
  • High loop or air gap to prevent backflow
  • Level installation and secure mounting

See dishwasher plumbing installation.

Refrigerator and ice maker

Needs a cold water line with a shutoff, ideally in a recessed box so the fridge can sit flush. Use quality braided or copper lines, and avoid crushing them. Water line leaks behind refrigerators are a classic hidden damage problem.

Garbage disposal

Disposal sizing, electrical switch and drain alignment all matter. See garbage disposal repair if you are replacing an old unit.

Gas

If you are switching to a gas range or cooktop, gas piping must be sized and installed by a licensed professional and pressure tested. If you smell gas, leave the home and call 911 or your gas utility first.

Kitchen Remodel Plumbing Problems and How We Prevent Them

Facebook and Reddit threads about San Antonio kitchens describe real damage: plumbing hidden behind ceilings, simple jobs padded with billable hours, water losses that wrecked floors and cabinets, and questionable workmanship discovered years later.

Common issues

  • Rubber or flexible drain hoses that sag and trap debris
  • No vent or wrong vent, causing gurgling and slow drains
  • Dishwasher drain without a high loop or air gap
  • Unpermitted work that surprises you at resale
  • Verbal quotes that grow when the job begins

  • Clear policy on change orders and unknown conditions
  • Photos of rough-in before closing
  • Pressure and drain testing
  • Clean installation with no kinked or crushed lines

San Antonio Seasons and Kitchen Remodels

Holiday season: Kitchens get the most use from Thanksgiving through New Year's. Start plumbing planning early so fixtures, cabinets and counters arrive on time. Heavy cooking also exposes grease clogs, so a remodel is a good time to clean or upgrade drains.

Winter cold snaps: Sink supply lines in exterior walls are vulnerable to freezing. Insulate and keep cabinet doors open during hard freezes. See frozen pipe repair.

Summer: Heat and humidity affect drying times for adhesives and finishes. Hot attics also make vent work harder.

Year-round hard water: Mineral scale shortens the life of faucets, dishwashers and ice-makers. A softener connection or RO system can protect your new investment.

Sinks, Faucets and Fixtures: What Affects the Plumbing

Sink types

  • Undermount and drop-in: Standard plumbing; cutout and cabinet specs matter.
  • Farmhouse (apron-front): Needs cabinet modification and careful drain alignment.
  • Double or offset-drain sinks: Change trap and disposal layout.
  • Workstation sinks: Add accessories and sometimes extra supply needs.

Faucets

Pull-down, pull-out and touchless faucets need adequate pressure and sometimes a power source. Pot fillers are cold-only, wall-mounted and require blocking and a dedicated line.

Disposals

Check horsepower and drain configuration. A double-bowl sink with disposal and dishwasher requires correct tailpiece and trap arrangement.

Water filtration

Under-sink filters and RO systems need a supply tee, drain connection and sometimes a dedicated faucet hole.

Why fixtures should be chosen first

Cabinet makers cut sink bases using sink templates. Plumbers rough in based on the faucet and sink. If they do not match, cabinets get modified, and that costs more than choosing early.

Small touches that prevent big problems

  • Use quarter-turn shutoffs under the sink
  • Install a drip tray or leak sensor in the sink cabinet
  • Leave a service gap behind the dishwasher for supply and drain access

Red flags

  • No clear scope or price

  • A plumber who has not looked at your layout

What good looks like

A clear written scope, photos of rough-in, test results, no surprise charges, and a plumber who returns calls.

Plumber or full remodeler?

If you only need plumbing changes, hire a plumber. If you need cabinets, counters, electrical and layout design, a remodeler may lead and bring in a plumber. Either way, ask who is responsible for plumbing scope, permits and testing. See our plumbing rough-in page for how timing works.

Why Kitchen Remodel Plumbing Should Not Be DIY

Swapping a faucet is DIY-friendly. Moving a sink, rerouting drains and venting are not.

Don’t DIY this. Kitchen remodel plumbing involves hidden pipes, code-required venting and concrete cutting. A mistake behind new cabinets can mean demolishing them to fix a leak.
  • Water damage: Leaks inside cabinets and walls cause mold and rot.
  • Code: Venting and slope must meet inspection standards.
  • Gas safety: Gas line errors can be dangerous.
  • Insurance and resale: Unpermitted work can complicate claims and sales.

You can still help by choosing fixtures, clearing cabinets and keeping the site accessible. Let a licensed plumber handle the pipes.

Kitchen Remodel Plumbing FAQs: Straight Answers for San Antonio Homeowners

Can a kitchen sink be moved to an island?

Yes. An island sink needs new supply lines, a sloped drain and a code-approved vent. On a slab foundation, this typically means cutting concrete, so it costs more than moving a sink along the same wall.

Do kitchen remodels require plumbing permits?

Moving or adding drain, vent or supply piping generally requires a permit in San Antonio. Simple like-for-like fixture swaps usually do not. We confirm requirements for your scope and handle permits for our work.

Can a dishwasher be relocated during remodeling?

Yes. It needs a hot water supply stop, a drain connection with a high loop or air gap, and electrical power. Moving it usually means new supply and drain routing.

When should I call a plumber for a kitchen remodel?

Before cabinets are ordered and before demolition covers existing pipes. Early input confirms what can move and what each change will cost.

How much does kitchen remodel plumbing cost?

It depends on how far fixtures move, your foundation type, pipe condition and any additions like a pot filler. We provide itemized written estimates after seeing your plan.

Can you add a pot filler or reverse osmosis system?

Yes. Both need dedicated supply lines and shutoffs, and a pot filler needs wall blocking. Plan these before drywall.

Will old pipes need replacing during the remodel?

If pipes are corroded, leaking or restricting flow, replacing them while walls are open is usually far cheaper than later repairs.

Can you work with my remodeler or designer?

Yes. We coordinate with contractors, designers and cabinet suppliers, and we also work directly with homeowners.

Do you handle gas lines for new ranges?

Gas work must be done by a licensed professional. Tell us about your range or cooktop plan so we can coordinate.
