Signs of a Damaged Sewer Line

  • Slow or gurgling drains throughout the house, not just one fixture.
  • Sewage odors inside the home or around the yard.
  • Wet, unusually green or sunken ground along the sewer path.
  • Sewage backing up into tubs, showers or floor drains.
  • Backups that return after drain cleaning or jetting.
  • Cracks in the slab or foundation near the line, or rodents and insects entering from a break.

If you see several of these, a camera inspection is the right first step. See Sewer Camera Inspection.

Common Causes of Sewer Pipe Failure

Sewer lines in San Antonio face a few predictable problems.

  • Expansive clay soil. Shifting soil separates joints, cracks pipe and creates sags where waste collects.
  • Tree roots. Roots enter aging cast iron, clay or PVC through joints and cracks.
  • Aged materials. Many older San Antonio homes, commonly those built before 1980, have corroded cast iron or Orangeburg pipe that cracks or collapses.
  • Heavy loads and construction. Driveways, vehicles and digging can crush shallow lines.

Not sure which of these matches your problem? Describe it and we will point you to the right fix.

Sewer Line Camera Diagnosis

The camera shows what is wrong, where it is and how far the damage runs. That is what separates an honest quote from a guess.

We locate the depth and position of the problem so repairs can target the damaged section instead of digging up the whole yard. Homeowners and plumbers agree on one thing: a clogged line is hard to inspect. Sometimes the line needs a cleaning first so the camera can see.

Ask to see what the camera sees. A good plumber shows you the footage and explains it.

Spot Repair vs. Full Replacement

Not every damaged sewer line needs to be replaced. Not every one can be saved with a patch either.

Spot repair or replacement?

One thing homeowners are told, and it is true: a liner cannot remove a belly or fix improper slope.

Trenchless and Conventional Repair Options

Where the pipe allows it, trenchless methods repair or replace a line with far less digging, protecting your yard, driveway and landscaping. Where the pipe has collapsed, is bellied or is too shallow, conventional excavation may be the only sound option.

The camera decides which approach fits. We explain both, so you are not told only the option that suits the contractor. See Trenchless Sewer Line Replacement.

Sewer Line Replacement Process

  1. Camera and locate to find the damage and its depth.
  2. Written estimate with the scope, method and restoration included.
  3. Permits and utility marking where required.
  4. Repair or replacement using the method the camera supports.
  5. Test and camera check to confirm the line is sound and flowing.
  6. Restoration of any dug areas and an explanation of what was done.

What Does Sewer Line Repair Cost in San Antonio?

Full replacement is commonly quoted between $3,000 and $15,000. Homeowners report quotes from about $8,000 up to $20,000 where depth, slabs or tunneling are involved.

That spread is the number one source of anxiety we hear. People are quoted $13,000 and wonder if it is real. Price depends heavily on length, depth, whether the line runs under concrete or a slab, and obstacles such as large trees. We show you the camera footage and the scope so the quote makes sense, and we put it in writing.

What moves the price

  • Length of line to repair or replace.
  • Depth and what is above it: lawn, driveway, concrete or the slab.
  • Repair method: spot repair, trenchless or open trench.
  • Permits, tunneling, trees and utility conflicts.

How to avoid overpaying

  • With a quote of this size, ask for camera footage and get a second written estimate if you have any doubt.
  • Ask for the price in writing before work starts, with labor, parts and any trip or diagnostic fee listed separately.
  • Ask the plumber to explain the cause in plain words. If you get a vague answer, get a second opinion.
  • Compare what is included, not just the headline price. A cheap special that grows into a long add-on list is not cheap.
  • Never let scope change on the fly. If something new is found, work should stop until you approve it.
  • Ask what happens if the problem comes back, and make sure anything promised is on the estimate.

What to do right now if you suspect a sewer line problem

  1. Limit water use: pause laundry, dishwasher and long showers.
  2. Do not use chemical drain openers on a main line problem.
  3. Find your cleanout, usually a capped pipe outside near the house.
  4. Note which fixtures are affected and when it started.
  5. Call us, and mention smells, wet ground or repeat backups.

Sewer Line Repair FAQs

What are the signs of a broken sewer line?

Multiple slow or gurgling drains, sewage smells, backups in tubs or floor drains, wet or sunken patches in the yard, and backups that return after cleaning. A camera inspection confirms what is happening and where.

Can tree roots damage sewer pipes?

Yes. Roots seek moisture and enter through joints and cracks in older cast iron, clay and even PVC pipe. Cutting roots clears the line for a while, but if the pipe is cracked, they come back.

When is sewer replacement necessary?

When the pipe is collapsed, badly bellied, corroded along its length, or repeatedly failing. If only one section is bad, a spot repair may be enough. A camera shows which case you have.

Will homeowners insurance pay for sewer line replacement?

Coverage depends on your policy and the cause. Many policies exclude wear and tear or tree roots. Read your policy, ask your insurer, and ask us for a written description of the findings to support a claim.

Do I need a permit to fix my sewer line?

Many sewer repairs and replacements need a permit, particularly work in the public right-of-way. We explain what your job needs before work begins.

Will I know the price before work starts?

Yes. You get a written estimate listing labor, parts and any trip or diagnostic fee, and we wait for your approval. If we find something that changes the scope, we stop and talk to you first.
