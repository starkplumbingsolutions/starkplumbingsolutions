What Is Backflow Testing?

Backflow assemblies protect the public water supply from contamination if pressure drops or reverses. Testing proves the check valves and relief valves work. One testing company states it directly: backflow testing is not a scam, it is needed to protect the water supply.

When Backflow Testing Is Required

SAWS monitors annual testing of backflow assemblies and says annual testing is required by Chapter 24 of the City of San Antonio’s Plumbing and Fuel Gas Code. Common assemblies are on irrigation systems, commercial buildings and fire lines.

San Antonio noteYour notice or SAWS account will tell you which assemblies are due. SAWS can confirm requirements for your property.

Testing by Qualified Professionals

Backflow tests must be done by a qualified tester. Ask for credentials and verify the license at tsbpe.texas.gov.

  • Ask for the tester’s license and certification
  • Ask how results are reported to SAWS
  • Ask whether repair and retest are included
  • Get the cost in writing

Backflow Testing FAQs for San Antonio Homeowners

Is annual backflow testing required for my property?

SAWS says annual testing of backflow assemblies is required under Chapter 24 of San Antonio’s Plumbing and Fuel Gas Code. Confirm your property with SAWS.

Who can test a backflow assembly in Texas?

A qualified, licensed tester. Verify credentials at tsbpe.texas.gov.

What happens if a preventer fails testing?

It is repaired or replaced and retested, and the passing result is reported.

How much does a backflow test cost?

It varies. Ask for the total including paperwork and any retest.

Where is my backflow assembly?

Often near the irrigation system or water meter.

Do I need testing for a sprinkler system?

Often yes, if it connects to the public water supply.
