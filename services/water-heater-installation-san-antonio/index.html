Water Heater Installation Services

We install and replace tank water heaters in gas and electric models, relocate units when a layout changes, and add the parts code often requires: expansion tanks on closed systems, pressure-reducing valves where supply pressure runs high, drain pans and T&P discharge piping. Removal and haul-away of the old unit can be included in the quote.

♨️

Gas vs. Electric Water Heaters

Gas tanks recover faster and often cost less to run where natural gas is already in place, but they need venting, combustion air and a properly sized gas line. Electric tanks have no venting and are simpler to install, but they draw more power and sometimes need a 240-volt circuit upgrade. Switching fuel types is possible in many homes. It adds gas or electrical work and sometimes a permit, so we quote it separately.

💧

Selecting Tank Size and Capacity

Tank size follows peak-hour demand, not just the number of people in the house. As a general guide, a small household often fits a 30 to 40 gallon tank, while a family of four or five with multiple showers usually needs 50 gallons or more. The first-hour rating (FHR) on the label shows how much hot water a unit delivers in the busiest hour. We size your unit on how you actually use hot water.

🔥

Replacing an Old Water Heater

Replacement starts with shutting down the old unit, draining it, disconnecting gas or power, and removing it safely. We check supply lines, shutoff valves and venting before the new unit goes in, because old fittings are a common source of later leaks. A tank that has leaked for a while may have damaged the floor pan or nearby drywall, which we flag in the estimate.

🧪

Permits, Code and Safety Testing

Water heater replacement often requires a permit, and any job involving a new gas line, venting change or electrical circuit almost always does. We explain what your job needs before work begins. After installation we check for leaks, confirm gas and venting are correct, set the thermostat to a safe temperature (120 degrees F is a common recommendation) and test the T&P valve.

⚖️

What a Replacement Costs in San Antonio

Published market ranges in San Antonio often run from about $1,900 to $2,500 for a standard tank replacement, and roughly $5,000 to $7,000 for many tankless installations. Attic placement, electrical upgrades, venting changes and code items move the total. Those figures are only a guide. Your written estimate is the number that applies to your home.

🛠️

Comparing Tank Warranties Before You Buy

Most tank heaters advertise a warranty, but the headline number rarely tells the whole story. Compare three things: how long the tank itself is covered, how long parts are covered, and whether labor is covered at all. A long tank warranty with a short parts warranty and no labor coverage means a failed element in year three can still cost you real money. Ask which party handles warranty claims, you or the installer, and whether the warranty stays valid if the installation is not done to code or by a licensed plumber.

We put the warranty terms in the estimate so you can compare options on equal footing, not just on sticker price.

📋

Attic, Garage and Closet Installs in San Antonio Homes

Many homes here have water heaters in the attic, in a garage corner or in a tight utility closet. Each location adds requirements. In an attic, a unit needs a safe path for service, a drain pan with a drain line to a visible discharge point, and a sturdy platform. Gas units in a garage need protection from impact and correct clearance from ignition sources. Closets need combustion air and proper venting.

Moving a heater to an easier location is possible, but it adds plumbing, venting or electrical work. We assess the current location first, explain what a like-for-like replacement involves, and quote relocation separately only if you want it.

🧰

Expansion Tanks, Pressure Regulators and Code Items on Quotes

When a quote includes an expansion tank, a pressure-reducing valve, a drain pan or new shutoff valves, it is usually because the existing setup would not pass inspection or would shorten the life of the new heater. A closed plumbing system cannot push expanding hot water back into the street, so the pressure rises unless an expansion tank absorbs it. High supply pressure stresses the tank and every fixture in the house.

These items are not padding. We tell you which are required, which are recommended and which you can decline, and you decide.

💡

Buying the Heater Yourself or Letting the Plumber Supply It

Some homeowners buy a unit from a home center and hire someone to set it. That can work, but it has tradeoffs. The unit must match your fuel type, venting and space, and a wrong model means a second trip. Warranty handling can get complicated when the buyer and installer are different parties, and a plumber may limit labor warranty on equipment they did not supply. If you choose to supply your own, tell us before the visit so we can confirm that the model fits and that we can install it to code.

🏠

Handyman or Licensed Plumber?

Neighborhood groups are full of offers to install a heater for a low flat labor price. The savings can disappear if the work lacks a permit, uses the wrong connectors or leaves gas and venting uncorrected. Insurance claims after a leak or fire can become difficult when work was unpermitted. Texas regulates plumbing, and you can verify a license with the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners at tsbpe.texas.gov. Ask for the license number and proof of liability insurance from anyone you hire, including us.

🔧

Same-Day Replacement: What Makes It Possible

A no-hot-water emergency does not wait for a convenient schedule. Same-day replacement depends on three things: the right unit being available, a straightforward location, and any permit needs being handled. We tell you honestly at the first call whether same-day is realistic for your situation, what is needed and what is not possible. If a gas line, venting or electrical upgrade is required, the job may take longer, and we explain why.

🔍

Heat Pump and High-Efficiency Options

Besides standard gas and electric tanks, some homes can use high-efficiency or heat pump water heaters that cut energy use. These need space, airflow and, in some cases, a condensate drain, and they cost more up front. Whether the savings justify the price depends on your usage and rates. Ask us to compare the options using your household's hot water habits, and we will give you the numbers, not a pitch.

♨️

Everyday Habits That Make a New Heater Last

Set the thermostat to a safe temperature, commonly 120 degrees Fahrenheit. Flush the tank on a schedule your installer recommends. Test the T&P valve yearly by lifting the lever briefly with a bucket under the discharge pipe, if your manufacturer permits it. Keep storage away from a gas unit. Watch the pan for moisture. A few small habits protect a major purchase.

💧

Planning Your Replacement Budget

A replacement has four cost pieces: the unit, the labor, the code and permit items, and any surprises behind the old heater such as corroded shutoff valves, damaged flooring or an undersized gas line. Ask for these separately so you can see where the money goes. Think about the cost over ten years, not just the purchase price: a unit with a better warranty and higher efficiency can cost less over time. If budget is tight, tell us. We can show a straightforward replacement, and separately list upgrades you can add later.

Plan ahead where you can. A heater that is noisy, rusting or past ten years is better replaced on your schedule than during a holiday weekend when you have no hot water.

🔥

Preparing Your Home for Installation Day

Clear a path from the door to the heater and a few feet around it. Move stored items out of the area, and secure pets. Tell us about steps, narrow doors and attic hatches in advance so we bring the right equipment. Expect water to be off for several hours. Plan showers and dishes around that window. Make sure someone over 18 is home to approve any scope change, and keep your phone nearby. After installation, we test the system, show you the shutoff valves and the relief valve, and explain the temperature setting and basic upkeep.

🧪

Gas, Venting and Electrical Work That May Come With Your Install

A swap is rarely just the tank. On gas units we check the gas line size, the shutoff valve, the sediment trap and the flexible connector, and we leak-test every joint. We confirm that the vent is sloped, sealed and clear, and that the draft hood or direct vent is in good shape. A weak vent can pull exhaust into the home, which is why venting is checked on every installation. On electric units we confirm the wire gauge, breaker size and a proper disconnect, and we tighten terminals, since loose connections create heat.

If anything fails inspection, we show you what we found and quote the correction separately before we continue. You decide whether to fix it now or later, and we tell you plainly which items are safety-critical.

Before the Plumber Arrives

Having these ready shortens the visit and helps us quote accurately.

  • Measure the space: width, depth, height and clearance
  • Confirm the fuel type and whether a gas line is nearby
  • Note the age and size of the current tank
  • Plan a clear path for the new unit to reach its location
  • Photograph the nameplate of the old unit
  • Decide early whether a tankless option fits your budget

Seasonal Issues in San Antonio

  • Spring: installing before summer avoids the rush when heat and hot water demand peak.
  • Storm season: plan for water heater access and power restoration after floods or outages.
  • Winter: units in unheated attics or garages need freeze protection and insulation.
  • Busy seasons: unit availability can tighten, so a planned replacement avoids a rushed choice on a dead tank.

Questions to Ask Any Plumber

Use these with every company you call, including us.

  • Is the permit included in your price?
  • What is the tank warranty, the parts warranty and the labor warranty?
  • Does the price include haul-away of the old unit?
  • Which code upgrades are required and which are optional?

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Picking a tank size only from the number of people at home
  • Skipping the drain pan in an attic or finished space
  • Letting an unlicensed installer connect gas or venting
  • Ignoring the thermal expansion tank on a closed system
  • Comparing quotes without checking what each includes
  • Waiting until the unit fails on a holiday weekend

Plain-English Glossary

First-hour rating

How much hot water a tank delivers in one busy hour.

Expansion tank

A small tank that absorbs pressure from heated water.

Drain pan

A pan under the tank that catches leaks and drains away.

Combustion air

The fresh air a gas unit needs to burn safely.

Recovery rate

How quickly a tank reheats after use.

PRV

Pressure-reducing valve that lowers incoming pressure.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my water heater needs replacement?

Common signs are a tank 10 years or older, leaks from the body, rust at the base, frequent repairs, and running out of hot water sooner than before. Age alone does not decide it. Repair cost and condition do.

Can I change from gas to electric?

Often yes, but it usually means new wiring, a 240-volt circuit, and removal of the gas line and venting. Some homes also need an electrical service upgrade. We price it separately after an inspection.

What factors determine water heater size?

Household size, peak-hour demand, number of bathrooms, fuel type and the first-hour rating all matter. A technician sizes the unit on how you use hot water.

How long does a water heater installation take?

A standard tank replacement is often completed in a few hours. Gas line, venting or electrical upgrades can add time, and the estimate explains what to expect.

Do I need a permit to replace a water heater in San Antonio?

Water heater replacement commonly requires a permit, and gas, venting or electrical changes almost always do. We explain what your job needs before work begins.

How much does water heater installation cost in San Antonio?

Published local ranges often run about $1,900 to $2,500 for a standard tank replacement. Attic placement, code upgrades and fuel changes can add to it. Your written estimate is the number that matters.

What size water heater do I need for my family?

Peak-hour demand decides it. Smaller homes often fit 30 to 40 gallons, while larger households often need 50 gallons or more. We size by how you use hot water.

How long does a water heater last?

Many tanks last around ten years, with some shorter or longer depending on water quality, maintenance and installation. Hard water shortens life if the tank is never flushed.
