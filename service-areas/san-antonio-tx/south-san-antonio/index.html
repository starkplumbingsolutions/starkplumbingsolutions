Common plumbing problems in South San Antonio

Because homes in South San Antonio are mostly mid-century slab-on-grade homes, the same few problems account for most calls. Here is what we see and why.

Slab leaks

Supply lines under the foundation corrode or shift with clay soil, causing warm floors and high bills.

Aging cast iron drains

Mid-century drains rust, scale and trap debris, producing repeat clogs.

High or unstable pressure

Original regulators fail and let street pressure strain fixtures.

Water heaters past their life

Tanks in this era often fail or leak after years of hard-water scale.

Outdoor faucet and yard leaks

Buried lines and old hose bibs leak quietly and raise the water bill.

Why these problems happen here

Many homes from the 1950s through the 1970s were built on slabs with supply lines in or beneath the concrete. Expansive clay soil swells with rain and shrinks in drought, which moves the foundation just enough to stress those lines. A slab leak may show up as a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water or a bill that jumps with no change in habits.

Drains of this age are usually cast iron or early PVC, and cast iron scales from the inside until the opening is a fraction of its original size. That is why a mid-century home can have slow drains everywhere even though no single clog is to blame. Leak detection and a camera inspection show which piece needs work.

Plumbing problems by season in South San Antonio

San Antonio’s climate is mild most of the year, then swings hard. Pick a season to see what to watch for in South San Antonio.

Winter plumbing in South San Antonio

Hard freezes are rare in San Antonio, which is why they cause so much damage: many pipes in attics, garages and exterior walls have little insulation. February 2021 left thousands of homes with burst lines. Insulate exposed pipe, disconnect hoses, cover outdoor faucets and know where your main shutoff is.

For mid-century slab-on-grade homes: A hard freeze is uncommon here, so slab homes with exposed hose bibs and attic lines are often unprepared. Cover outdoor faucets and insulate exposed pipe.

Spring plumbing in South San Antonio

Spring brings storms, flash flooding and shifting soil. Heavy rain can expose slow sewer lines and overwhelm yard drainage. Check for slow drains, wet spots in the yard and any new cracks near the foundation.

For mid-century slab-on-grade homes: Spring rain swells clay soil and can shift lines. Heavy storms also expose slow sewer joints.

Summer plumbing in South San Antonio

Triple-digit heat and drought dry out our clay soil, which shrinks and shifts foundations and stresses buried lines. Irrigation drives water use higher, so a high bill is a clue worth checking against the meter.

For mid-century slab-on-grade homes: Summer drought dries the soil and shifts slabs, which is when many slab leaks begin. Watch the meter and the bill.

Fall plumbing in South San Antonio

Fall is the best maintenance season. Flush the water heater, test shutoffs, check hose bibs and clean drains before holiday hosting.

For mid-century slab-on-grade homes: Fall is a good time to flush the water heater and test pressure before heavy winter use.

Check for a hidden leak in 5 minutes

A simple meter test finds many hidden leaks without any tools. First, turn off every faucet, appliance and irrigation zone, and make sure no one is using water. Next, find your water meter, usually in a box near the street or curb, and lift the lid carefully. Look at the small leak indicator, often a triangle or dial, and note the reading. If the indicator moves while everything is off, water is escaping somewhere. Wait fifteen minutes and check again. If the reading has changed, close the main shutoff and see whether the movement stops. If it stops, the leak is inside the house. If it continues, the leak is between the meter and the house. Either way, do not start digging or breaking into walls. Call a licensed plumber so the leak can be located and repaired without extra damage, and photograph the meter readings for your records.
