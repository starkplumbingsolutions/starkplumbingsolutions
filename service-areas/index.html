Plumbing problems by season in San Antonio

Our climate is mild most of the year, then swings hard. Heat, drought, sudden freezes and spring storms each stress plumbing in different ways. Here is what to watch for and when.

Winter plumbing in San Antonio

Hard freezes are rare in San Antonio, which is why they hurt: many pipes in attics, garages and exterior walls are barely insulated. February 2021 left thousands of homes with burst lines. Before a forecast freeze, insulate exposed pipes, disconnect hoses, cover outdoor faucets, open cabinet doors under sinks, let faucets drip and know where your main shutoff is. If a pipe freezes, do not use a torch or open flame. Shut off the main and call us.

Emergency frozen pipe repair →

Spring plumbing in San Antonio

Storms and saturated soil push roots into older sewer lines and overload drains. Expect slow tubs, gurgling toilets and yard wet spots. Spring is the best time for a camera inspection of any pre-1980 sewer line, and for checking sprinkler backflow devices and outdoor faucets before irrigation season.

Sewer camera inspection →

Summer plumbing in San Antonio

Triple-digit heat means high water use, drought-cracked soil and shifting foundations, which is the classic setup for slab leaks. Watch your water bill and meter. Heat also accelerates scale in water heaters and stresses pressure regulators. A pressure over 80 psi is hard on every fixture in the house.

Slab leak repair →

Fall plumbing in San Antonio

Fall is the time for water heater service before guests, holidays and the first cold snap: flush the tank, test the relief valve, check the anode condition and inspect for rust at the base. It is also the time to clear exterior drains and gutters, and to plan any repipe or remodel plumbing before the busy winter season.

When not to do it yourself

Many San Antonio homeowners are capable and handy, and a few plumbing jobs are fine to do yourself, such as replacing a toilet flapper, cleaning a faucet aerator or plunging a single sink. The jobs below carry real risk of flooding, gas exposure, injury, or a denied insurance claim.

  • Gas lines and gas water heatersA bad connection leaks gas. Gas work requires a licensed plumber and a pressure test.
  • Sewer lines and main drainsRented augers can tear old cast iron or clay pipe and leave you with a bigger repair.
  • Slab leaksGuessing where the leak is means breaking concrete twice. Electronic detection finds it first.
  • Water heater replacementRelief valves, venting, expansion tanks and permits matter. Errors can scald, flood or void warranties.
  • Anything inside a wallSoldering, fitting and testing behind drywall without a pressure test often turns a small leak into mold.
  • Chemical drain openersThey rarely clear roots or grease deep in the line, and they put caustic liquid in the pipe your plumber must then handle.

Local knowledge that shapes how we diagnose

Three local factors explain most of the plumbing calls we see. First is soil. San Antonio sits on clay that swells when wet and shrinks in drought, and that movement is a leading reason pipes under slabs fail. Second is water. Local water is hard, so scale builds inside water heaters, shower valves and dishwashers. Third is age. Central neighborhoods have drain lines older than most homeowners, while newer northwest subdivisions have builder-grade fixtures reaching replacement age. When you call, tell us your neighborhood and the year your home was built. It helps us arrive with the right tools and gives you a more accurate expectation.

How much does a plumber cost in San Antonio?

It depends on the problem, access, parts and timing. A faucet or drain job costs far less than slab leak repair or sewer replacement. We give a written estimate listing labor, parts and any diagnostic fee, and we wait for your approval before starting.

The list covers the San Antonio neighborhoods that have their own pages. If your address, suburb or nearby city isn’t listed, call (210) 898-9754 or send us your address and we’ll tell you honestly whether we can get to you.

How do I check that a San Antonio plumber is licensed?

Texas plumbers are licensed by the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners. Ask any plumber, including us, for a license number and look it up at tsbpe.texas.gov. Also ask for proof of liability insurance before work starts.

Why do older San Antonio homes have so many drain problems?

Homes built before the 1960s often have cast iron or clay sewer lines. Roots, joint separation and corrosion cause repeat clogs. A sewer camera inspection shows the actual condition so you can choose between cleaning, spot repair or replacement.

What causes slab leaks in San Antonio?

Expansive clay soil swells when wet and shrinks in drought, which can shift a foundation and stress pipes under it. Corrosion and high water pressure add to the risk. Warning signs are warm floor spots, a high water bill and the sound of running water.

Can hard water damage my plumbing in San Antonio?

Yes. San Antonio water is hard, and mineral scale builds up inside water heaters, faucets and appliances, which shortens their life. Flushing the heater yearly and considering a softener helps. We can size one for your household.

Should I fix a plumbing problem myself?

Small jobs like replacing a flapper or a faucet washer are reasonable. Gas lines, sewer lines, slab leaks, water heater replacement and anything behind a wall should go to a licensed plumber. A mistake can cause flooding, gas hazards or an insurance denial.

Does Stark Plumbing Solutions serve commercial properties?

Yes. We handle plumbing for restaurants, offices, retail and multi-unit buildings, including drains, water heaters, leaks and backflow-related work. Call for a site visit and written estimate.

What should I do before the plumber arrives?

Shut off the main water valve if water is escaping, turn off the water heater, move belongings away from water, avoid electrical outlets near water and take photos for insurance. The checklist on this page walks you through it.

Is there help with emergency leak repair costs in San Antonio?

The San Antonio Water System runs a Plumbers to People program that helps qualifying low-income residential customers with leak repair. Call 210-704-SAVE or visit saws.org for eligibility. Stark is not affiliated with SAWS.
