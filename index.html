Plumbing Repairs for Leaks, Pipes and Fixtures

Most plumbing repair calls in San Antonio start small: a faucet that won’t stop dripping, a toilet that keeps running, a shower valve that’s gone stiff. Left alone, they waste water and quietly raise your SAWS bill. The good news is that these are usually quick, inexpensive fixes when they’re caught early.

When you call about plumbing repair in San Antonio, it helps to know three things: where the water is, whether you can shut it off at the fixture, and how long it’s been happening. Those answers tell us whether we’re looking at a worn washer, a failed supply line or something behind the wall.

Signs a small leak needs attention now

  • Water stains, bubbling paint or soft drywall near a bathroom or kitchen
  • A cabinet under the sink that smells musty or feels damp
  • Pipes that knock, whistle or shake when water runs
  • Green or white crust on fittings, a sign of slow seepage and mineral buildup

If water is actively spraying or flooding, skip the reading and use the emergency steps below, or call (210) 898-9754.

Drains & sewers

Drain Cleaning and Sewer Line Services

A single slow drain is usually a local clog. When several drains slow down together, or water backs up somewhere it shouldn’t, the problem is more likely in the main line. That difference decides whether you need drain cleaning in San Antonio or a closer look at the sewer line.

Call a professional if you notice

  • More than one fixture draining slowly, or gurgling when another fixture runs
  • Water coming up in a tub or shower when you flush or run the washer
  • A sewage smell indoors or in the yard, or unusually green, soggy patches of lawn
  • A clog that returns days after plunging or snaking

A camera inspection shows what’s actually in the line, such as grease, tree roots, a belly or a crack, so the repair matches the problem instead of guesswork. For recurring grease and buildup, hydro jetting scours the pipe wall rather than punching a hole through the clog. For damaged lines, trenchless methods can often avoid digging up the whole yard.

About chemical drain cleaners: a bottle of cleaner can work on a mild hair clog, but repeated use can damage older pipes, and standing caustic water is hazardous for whoever works on the drain next. If one round didn’t work, stop and call.

Water Heater Repair, Installation and Replacement

No hot water is one of the fastest ways to ruin a morning, and it’s also one of the most fixable. Whether you need water heater repair in San Antonio or a full replacement, the first question is the same: repair it or replace it?

Repair when…

The unit is relatively new and the issue is a heating element, thermostat, pilot or thermocouple, or a leaking valve or fitting. Repairs are typically the lower-cost route.

Water heater repair →

Replace when…

Water leaks from the tank body, the heater is roughly a decade old or more, you see rusty hot water, or repairs keep repeating. Replacement avoids a flood and an emergency call.

Consider tankless if…

You want endless hot water, more floor space and lower standby heat loss. Sizing, gas supply and venting matter, so we walk you through the setup before you commit.

Emergency Plumbing Problems

The first few minutes decide how much damage you’ll have. Choose your situation for what to do while you wait. For urgent help, call (210) 898-9754 or see emergency plumber San Antonio.

Burst or frozen pipe

  1. Shut off the main water valve. It’s typically near the street meter or where the line enters your home. Turn it clockwise until it stops.
  2. Turn off the water heater’s power or gas so it doesn’t run dry.
  3. Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain the remaining water and relieve pressure.
  4. If water is near outlets, the panel or ceiling light fixtures, stay clear. Only switch off power at the breaker if you can reach it without standing in water.
  5. Move valuables, take photos for insurance and mop up standing water.

Burst pipes don’t wait. Call now and tell us what you’ve already shut off.

Call (210) 898-9754

Burst pipe repair · Frozen pipes · Main line

Overflowing toilet

  1. Stop flushing. Lift the tank lid and press the rubber flapper down to stop more water entering the bowl.
  2. Turn the shutoff valve behind or beside the toilet clockwise to close it.
  3. Put down towels, and wear gloves if the overflow is from the drain rather than the tank.
  4. Try a flange plunger once. If it doesn’t clear, or other drains are acting up too, stop and call. Don’t pour in chemical cleaners.

If more than one drain is slow, the clog may be in the main line, not the toilet.

Sewer backup

  1. Stop using water: no flushing, showers, laundry or dishwasher.
  2. Keep children and pets away. Sewage water carries bacteria, so wear gloves and boots if you must go in.
  3. Don’t use chemical cleaners. They won’t clear a main line and add hazards.
  4. Photograph the damage for insurance, ventilate if it’s safe, and call.

Backups in the lowest drains (tub, shower, floor drain) usually point to the main sewer line.

Water heater leaking or no hot water

  1. Leaking: shut the cold-water supply valve on top of the heater, then turn off the electricity at the breaker or set the gas control to “off.”
  2. Don’t touch scalding water or the pressure-relief discharge pipe.
  3. No hot water: check the breaker (electric) or whether the pilot is lit (gas). If a reset doesn’t help, call.
  4. Run a drain hose to a floor drain or outdoors if there’s a lot of water.

A leaking tank can’t be repaired, but it can usually be replaced quickly.

Hidden leaks

Water Leak Detection and Slab Leak Repair

The leaks that cost the most are the ones you can’t see. A hidden leak in a wall, yard line or under the slab can run for weeks before the first stain shows, and the first hint is often a water bill that jumps with no change in how you live.

San Antonio’s shifting clay soils and aging pipes in older neighborhoods make slab and underground leaks a real local issue. Water leak detection in San Antonio uses acoustic listening, thermal imaging, pressure testing and moisture meters to pinpoint the exact spot, so the repair is targeted and not a demolition job. If the leak is under the foundation, see slab leak repair.

Do this 15-minute meter test

  • Turn off every faucet and appliance that uses water, including the ice maker.
  • Open your water meter box and look at the small leak-indicator dial or triangle.
  • If it’s spinning, or the numbers change after 15–30 minutes with everything off, water is going somewhere.

Also check for a running toilet first. It’s the most common false alarm, and a few drops of food coloring in the tank will show a leak into the bowl.

Local knowledge

Plumbing Considerations for San Antonio Properties

San Antonio plumbing has its own quirks. Knowing them helps you spot problems early and ask smarter questions.

Hard, mineral-heavy water

Much of the city’s water comes from the Edwards Aquifer and is naturally hard. Limescale clogs aerators, shortens water heater life and stiffens valves. Softeners and filtration help: see softener installation and whole-house filtration.

Occasional hard freezes

Freezes are rare here, so many homes and outdoor lines aren’t insulated for them. When one hits, exposed pipes and hose bibs burst. Know where your main shutoff is before it happens. See frozen pipe repair.

Shifting clay soil and slab leaks

Expansive clay moves with wet and dry cycles and can stress pipes under or around the foundation. Rising bills, warm floor spots and the sound of running water are classic signs. See slab leak repair.

Aging pipes in older neighborhoods

Homes in areas like King William, Alamo Heights, Monte Vista and Tobin Hill may still have galvanized or cast-iron lines. Consider repiping if leaks keep appearing.

What affects plumber prices in San Antonio?

Nobody can quote an honest number for a plumbing job sight-unseen. What we can do is tell you what moves the price, then give you a written estimate once we’ve seen the problem, so you can compare quotes fairly:

  • The problem itself: clearing a sink drain costs far less than locating a slab leak or replacing a sewer line.
  • Access: work behind finished walls, under slabs or deep underground takes longer.
  • Parts and materials: standard fixtures versus specialty or high-efficiency equipment.
  • Timing: nights, weekends and holidays usually cost more than a scheduled visit.
  • Pricing method: flat-rate per job versus hourly, and whether a diagnostic or trip fee is credited to the repair.

Whoever you call, get the estimate in writing and compare it line by line. The cheapest number isn’t always the lowest final bill.

Need help paying for a leak?

The San Antonio Water System (SAWS) runs a Plumbers to People program that provides emergency water leak repair assistance to qualifying low-income residential customers. Call 210-704-SAVE or visit saws.org to ask about eligibility and availability.

Plan ahead

Seasonal Plumbing Tips for San Antonio Homes

San Antonio’s weather puts different stress on your plumbing each season. We open on the current season, but tap any tab to plan ahead.

Spring: rain, drainage and outdoor plumbing (Mar–May)

  • Heavy spring storms test drainage. Check that gutters and downspouts carry water well away from the foundation.
  • Test your sump pump by pouring water into the pit, and check that the discharge line is clear.
  • Inspect outdoor faucets and irrigation lines for winter damage: leaks, drips or low pressure.
  • Have irrigation backflow devices tested as required.
  • Check under sinks and around toilets for slow leaks, soft cabinet bottoms and mildew.
  • Look at washing machine hoses for bulges or cracks, and replace aging rubber hoses.

Spring is the best time to fix small leaks and drainage problems before summer heat.

Summer: high water use and dry soil (Jun–Aug)

  • Watch your water bill. Irrigation season makes leaks easy to hide, so run the meter test if the bill spikes.
  • Dry, cracking clay soil can shift foundations. Warm floor spots or the sound of running water can signal a slab leak.
  • Tree roots hunt for water. Recurring slow drains can mean roots in the sewer line.
  • Flush your water heater tank to clear hard-water sediment.
  • Before vacation, shut off the washing machine valves, or the main if you’ll be away for a while.
  • Check hose bibs and sprinkler heads for leaks that waste water all season.

Summer water use is the highest of the year. A small leak costs the most now.

Fall: get ready for cold and holidays (Sep–Nov)

  • Winterize before the first freeze: disconnect hoses, shut off and drain outdoor lines, and insulate exposed pipes.
  • Have your water heater checked before the cold arrives, and flush it if it hasn’t been done this year.
  • Clear gutters so fall rains drain away from your foundation.
  • Test your main and fixture shutoff valves. A valve that won’t turn is useless in an emergency.
  • Hosting for the holidays? Keep grease, coffee grounds and fibrous scraps out of drains and disposals.
  • If an older home keeps springing leaks, ask about repiping before winter stress hits old lines.

Fall is the ideal time to winterize, and the busiest season for holiday drain clogs.

Winter: protect pipes from freezes (Dec–Feb)

  • Insulate exposed pipes in attics, garages and outside walls, and cover outdoor faucets.
  • Disconnect garden hoses. A hose left on a hose bib can freeze and split the pipe inside the wall.
  • Know where your main shutoff is before a freeze.
  • On hard-freeze nights, let faucets drip and open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls.
  • Heading out of town? Keep the heat on and consider shutting off the main.
  • If a pipe freezes, shut off the main and thaw gently with warm air. Never use an open flame.

San Antonio freezes are rare, which is exactly why so many pipes aren’t protected for them.

What should I do if a water pipe bursts?

Shut off the main water valve right away, turn off the water heater’s power or gas, and open the lowest faucet to drain remaining water. Stay clear of water near outlets or electrical panels, move belongings out of the way, take photos for insurance, and call us at (210) 898-9754.

How do I know whether I have a hidden plumbing leak?

Common signs are an unexplained jump in your water bill, the sound of running water when everything is off, damp or warm spots on floors, a musty smell, and a water meter that keeps moving with all fixtures off. Try the 15-minute meter test, and if it moves, schedule leak detection.

Can you repair or replace a water heater?

Yes. A failed heating element, thermostat, pilot or valve can often be repaired. A tank that leaks from the body, rusty hot water, repeated repairs or a heater around ten years old or more usually points to replacement. We can also discuss tankless water heaters for homes with suitable gas and venting.

When does a clogged drain require professional attention?

Call a plumber when more than one fixture drains slowly, water backs up in a tub or shower when you flush or run the washer, you hear gurgling or smell sewage, or a clog keeps coming back after plunging. These signs often point to a main line problem, not a simple sink clog.
